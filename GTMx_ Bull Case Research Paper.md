## **1\. Executive summary**

GTMx is a two-founder, US-registered go-to-market agency that positions itself not as an outbound shop but as a revenue infrastructure engineering firm. Its central claim is architectural: most B2B companies buy outbound, RevOps and search from three different vendors and end up with three disconnected systems. GTMx sells all three as one connected engine, built inside the client's own accounts, with an explicit hand-over rather than a lock-in.

The bull case rests on six things:

1. It is positioned on the **only defensible axis left** in a crowded category: integration across three functions rather than excellence within one.  
2. It has an **early AEO wedge** (getting cited by ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini) at exactly the moment buyer research is migrating to answer engines.  
3. Its case study numbers are **specific, modest and internally consistent**, and are now corroborated by four full first-person client transcripts.  
4. **It acquires its own clients with its own product.** At least two of the four documented clients were won by cold email sent by the founder. That is the strongest possible proof of the service.  
5. It carries **six named tooling partnerships** across competing vendors, a disproportionate credibility and referral surface for a firm this young.  
6. Its **anti-lock-in model** converts the category's biggest buyer objection into a sales asset.

## **3\. Positioning**

The tagline is "Outbound, RevOps, and Search, built into one engine", carried with the sub-claim that competitors duct-tape tools and agencies together and call the result a go-to-market.

Everything on the site is built to support that one idea. The three service lines share a single delivery method, a single audit entry point and a single commercial promise (you own what we build). The firm is not selling three services. It is selling the connection between them, and it treats the disconnection as the client's actual problem.

The target band is deliberate: B2B SaaS and agencies at \$1M+ ARR. That is companies past product-market fit, with revenue to invest, but without the internal bandwidth or seniority to build GTM infrastructure well. It sits below the revenue floors of the top-tier Clay agencies and above the commodity SDR shops.

## **4\. Service line one: Automated Outbound**

Done-for-you cold email and LinkedIn, built, launched and run end to end.

**Four components:**

1. **Email infrastructure.** Secondary domains that redirect to the client's primary, warmed inboxes, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, and ongoing deliverability monitoring. Sending never touches the primary domain. Domains warm over two to three weeks and sending rotates across multiple mailboxes to keep volume safe.  
2. **Targeting and data.** ICP and TAM modelling, list building and enrichment in Clay, scored and prioritised on intent and fit signals rather than bought lists.  
3. **Personalised copy.** Multi-angle sequences drafted with AI and edited by humans, A/B tested each cycle.  
4. **Multi-channel distribution.** Cold email and LinkedIn running simultaneously, with reply triage, lead qualification, meeting booking and clean CRM sync.

**Timeline:** roughly three weeks to launch. Week one is infrastructure and ICP research, week two is copy and go-live, week three onward is iteration on live results. First qualified meetings typically land inside 30 days.

**Qualification bar:** proven product-market fit, fast lead follow-up, a TAM north of roughly 20,000 accounts, and an average deal value that justifies a meeting.

**Client visibility:** lead lists, sequences and copy variants are all approved by the client before launch. Once live, a shared dashboard carries daily reply data, booked meetings and pipeline value. Client transcripts confirm Slack as the running communication channel, with check-ins every couple of weeks on targeting adjustments.

**Default stack:** Clay for enrichment, Instantly for cold email, HeyReach for LinkedIn, HubSpot or Salesforce for CRM. Builds have also run on Attio and Pipedrive.

## **5\. Service line two: RevOps**

Sold as "GTM engineering as a service": custom Clay and CRM builds that give sales and marketing the infrastructure they are missing.

**Four components:**

1. **TAM sourcing.** Building and segmenting the total addressable market into clean, prioritised account lists.  
2. **Inbound orchestration.** Form fills and sign-ups enriched, scored, qualified and routed within seconds of landing.  
3. **CRM enrichment.** HubSpot or Salesforce kept clean and complete via Clay and 120+ data providers wired into the stack.  
4. **Lead scoring.** Surfacing the accounts and contacts most likely to convert.

**Two commercial models:** scoped sprint projects for a single use case (TAM sourcing, inbound orchestration, CRM enrichment, or a HubSpot build), or an ongoing subscription with dedicated engineers who adapt to client priorities week to week.

**ICP:** tech companies roughly between Seed and Series C, or service businesses with a real sales motion, that need GTM infrastructure built and lack the internal bandwidth to build it well.

**Success metrics**, baselined in week one and reported weekly:

* **Time saved:** hours per week returned to the team from manual work  
* **Data quality:** deduplication rate, enrichment coverage, lead-to-account match rate  
* **Pipeline velocity:** speed from new lead to qualified

**Hand-over:** every workflow is built in the client's tools, on the client's account, under the client's login. Hand-over includes documentation, Loom walkthroughs and a 30-day support window.

## **6\. Service line three: SEO \+ AEO**

Ranking on Google and being cited by AI answer engines, treated as two distinct disciplines run together.

**Four components:**

1. **Technical SEO.** Site architecture, Core Web Vitals, crawlability and schema.  
2. **Content at scale.** Researched content via a human-in-the-loop AI workflow, built on a citable framework, aimed at high-intent buy-ready queries.  
3. **Answer engine optimisation.** Entity and knowledge-graph work, structured data, and content engineered for AI systems to cite.  
4. **Authority and citations.** Digital PR, community and Reddit presence, and the trusted sources models pull from, plus tracking that ties rankings and LLM citations back to pipeline.

**Six-step delivery:** audit, query mapping, technical foundation, content production, authority and citations, then ongoing optimisation.

**Platforms optimised for:** ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, plus emerging answer engines as they gain real buyer traffic.

**Timelines:** AI visibility moves first, often within the first week or two. First indexed pages and rank movement at four to six weeks. Meaningful organic traffic by around month three. Compounding pipeline impact from month six onward.

**AEO measurement framework:** citation frequency across the four major models for the queries buyers actually run, branded mentions, comparison appearances ("X vs Y"), and category-defining citations ("best tools for X"), all tied back to organic traffic, demo requests and pipeline.

**On AI-written content:** AI handles keyword research, gap analysis and first-pass drafts. A senior strategist sets the angle, fact-checks and rewrites for tone. Nothing ships without human review.

**On guarantees:** GTMx explicitly refuses to guarantee rankings, and states that anyone offering such a guarantee is either lying or planning to spam. What it guarantees instead is the system: technical baseline, content shipped on schedule, citation tracking in place, monthly performance review.

## **7\. The GTMx Method**

A single four-step loop applied identically across all three service lines.

| Step | Automated Outbound | RevOps | SEO \+ AEO |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **1\. Audit** | ICP, domain health, outbound stack, reply data | CRM, data flow, enrichment coverage, routing logic | Technical SEO, content coverage, backlinks, AI visibility versus competitors |
| **2\. Build** | Warmed domains, sending infrastructure, Clay intent lists, sequences, LinkedIn profiles | Clay enrichment workflows, lead scoring, dedup, inbound routing, CRM setup | Programmatic templates, keyword clusters mapped to intent, technical fixes, briefs for both search and LLMs |
| **3\. Launch** | Campaigns live across email and LinkedIn at once, replies managed, meetings booked | System switched on end to end, team trained, dashboards live | Pages ship at scale, technical SEO live, indexation and citations monitored from day one |
| **4\. Iterate** | Weekly tuning on copy variants, segment performance, reply quality | Weekly reviews, scoring model tuned, routing refined, new workflows shipped | Weekly tuning on what ranks and gets cited, decayers refreshed, adjacent clusters added |

Timelines differ by line. Outbound audits in week one and launches in weeks three to four. RevOps and search both build across weeks two to four and launch weeks four to six. All three move into iteration from month two.

The strategic value is not the framework itself, which is unremarkable. It is that one loop across three service lines makes the business trainable and therefore scalable. A firm where every engagement runs the same operating rhythm can hire delivery staff against a documented process rather than against founder intuition.

## **12\. Content and point of view**

Three published articles, all bottom-of-funnel, all arguing one thesis from different angles.

**The \$250K Mistake: Why Hiring a VP of Sales First Doesn't Work** (18 March 2026). A VP of Sales costs \$200-300K fully loaded, takes three to six months to ramp, and nearly half of first senior sales hires are replaced within two years. The argument is that the hire is asked to do three jobs at once (define the ICP, build the infrastructure, generate pipeline) and cannot do all three well. The alternative sequence: spend 60 to 90 days building outbound infrastructure, validate the ICP through actual responses, book the first 15 to 30 qualified meetings, then hire. Ramp drops from six months to six weeks.

**Using AI to Build Your First Outbound Pipeline** (25 March 2026). The practical stack, costed. Clay for enrichment, Instantly for cold email, HeyReach for LinkedIn, Claude as the AI layer for research and reply handling, HubSpot for CRM. Under \$1,000 per month in tooling, three to four weeks to first qualified meeting. The core argument is that AI's value in outbound is research, not writing: use it to mine how target companies describe their own problems, then write in that language.

**Why Your Current Sales Playbook Won't Scale** (1 April 2026). Referral-led motions break on cold segments because three assumptions differ: relationship (cold buyers expect to be found, the relationship comes after the first meeting), messaging (capability-focused pitches get archived, outcome-focused ones land), and urgency (cold buyers move faster on ROI and ghost faster without it, so warm-lead cadences read as passive).

The VP of Sales piece is the strongest commercial writing on the site because it attacks the **substitute product**, a \$250K hire, rather than competing agencies. That is category-creation writing rather than blog filler.

Notably, the published thesis and the client transcripts agree. The articles argue that cold buyers need short, direct, outcome-led messaging; Bill at United Safety independently describes abandoning long detailed emails for short direct ones and watching lead flow arrive. The content is documenting practice, not inventing positioning.

## **14\. Bull case pillars**

### **Pillar 1: Positioned on the last defensible axis**

The GTM engineering category is saturated. A single market scan surfaces ColdIQ, The Kiln, Understory, DevCommX, GTME, Reachly, Viralnetix, LeadGem and OneGTM, most running near-identical stacks (Clay plus Instantly or Smartlead plus HeyReach plus HubSpot) and near-identical messaging ("systems not campaigns", "you own the infrastructure").

When everyone competes on the same axis, that axis stops differentiating. GTMx has stepped sideways. It is not claiming to run better outbound. It is claiming to be the only firm running outbound, RevOps and search as one connected system. Competitors that also touch search treat it as a bolt-on. GTMx gives it its own method, timeline and FAQ set as a co-equal third pillar. That is a genuinely under-occupied position.

### **Pillar 2: The firm eats its own cooking**

This is the pillar the transcripts unlock, and it is the most persuasive one in the paper.

**Two of the four documented clients were acquired by cold email sent by the founder.** Pascal at Strategy Achievers remembers the specific creative hook. Bill at United Safety was reached the same way. In both cases the prospect was a senior decision maker who receives constant outbound and who nonetheless replied.

Every agency in this category claims outbound works. Almost none can point to their own client list as the proof. GTMx can say, to a prospect, that this conversation exists because the system being sold produced it. That collapses the entire credibility problem in one sentence and it is not copyable by any competitor that bought its clients through referrals or paid ads.

### **Pillar 3: Demonstrated remediation capability, not just build capability**

The Vidify engagement is a turnaround, not a greenfield build. Three agencies had already failed. The fixes were specific and technical: strip open tracking, remove images and HTML, simplify copy, restore deliverability.

That matters commercially because the largest addressable segment in outbound is not companies that have never tried. It is companies that have tried and failed, hold burned domains and a sceptical CEO, and need someone who can diagnose why. GTMx has a documented, named, on-record case of doing exactly that. "Three agencies before us" is the highest-value sentence in the entire evidence base.

### **Pillar 4: The AEO wedge is early and correctly timed**

Two structural reasons this matters.

**Search behaviour is migrating.** A growing share of vendor research now starts inside an AI tool rather than a search bar. Firms optimising only for classic Google become invisible to that traffic.

**AEO compounds faster than SEO.** First AI citations at two to four weeks versus rank movement at four to six, because models re-index more frequently than Google re-ranks. Faster proof means shorter time-to-value, which means better retention on a service line that historically churns before it works.

The compounding point: an agency that can get its clients cited by language models can get itself cited by language models. That is an inbound channel with no ad spend, which is precisely what a two-person firm needs.

### **Pillar 5: Anti-lock-in converts the biggest objection into a close**

The category's dominant buyer fear is dependency: pay a retainer, build nothing, lose everything when the contract ends. GTMx makes client ownership the structural spine of the pitch rather than a bullet point, and pairs it with a direct comparison to hiring: a senior RevOps hire takes four to six months to ramp and three more to ship a meaningful workflow, versus 30 to 60 days for the same system, with the eventual in-house hire inheriting something already working.

That reframes the purchase from cost centre to **hire de-risking**, which unlocks a different budget line and a different approver.

### **Pillar 6: Predictability is the retained value, and clients say so unprompted**

Bill's testimony is the clearest articulation of what the service actually sells. Not leads. **Consistency.** Once response rates are known and hold, the business can forecast and scale against them. He credits the firm with hitting stated numbers reliably.

That is a retention argument. Lead volume is a transactional purchase and churns. Forecastability is an operating dependency and renews. A client who has hired staff against expected lead flow, as United Safety did, has structurally embedded the service into their planning.

### **Pillar 7: Technical sophistication that an SDR team cannot replicate**

The OpenSponsorship campaign (scrape brand SKUs, match to marketplace athletes, generate per-prospect collaboration pitches) is a marketplace-matching data play, not a mail merge. It justifies the engineering framing and it sets a price floor: work of that kind cannot be competed away by cheaper labour, only by comparable capability.

### **Pillar 8: Qualification discipline signals a retention-first business**

GTMx publicly disqualifies prospects: no product-market fit, no fast follow-up, a TAM under roughly 20,000 accounts, or a deal value that does not justify a meeting, and the engagement is not a fit. It also refuses to guarantee rankings on the search side, and sets expectations explicitly with clients that it books qualified interest rather than closed deals.

An agency that turns business away at the top of the funnel and lowers expectations at the point of sale is optimising for engagement outcomes over signup volume. That is the opposite of the category norm.

### **Pillar 9: Multi-vendor partnerships enable fit-based recommendation**

Holding Smartlead, Instantly and EmailBison partnerships simultaneously means GTMx is not locked into recommending one sending platform. Combined with CRM flexibility across HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio and Pipedrive, the firm can architect around the client's existing stack rather than forcing a migration. That materially widens the addressable set.

### **Pillar 10: Cross-vertical proof, not single-niche proof**

The four documented clients span athlete marketing (marketplace), personal branding (agency), AI video generation (SaaS-adjacent agency) and violence prevention training (services, public sector adjacent). Buyer titles range from CMOs to Chief HR Officers to school and hospital administrators.

An agency with four wins in one vertical has a niche. An agency with four wins across four unrelated verticals has a **method**. The latter is far more scalable and far more defensible in a sales conversation with a prospect from a fifth vertical.
