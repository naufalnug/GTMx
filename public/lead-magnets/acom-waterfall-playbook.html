01 — Signal

The Language Coverage Gap

Three free public datasets nobody joins: census language data, the organisation's own published language obligation, and what their campaigns actually ran in. The join names the gap. ACoM alone can name the outlets that close it.

strongest signal
01

It is produced by law and demography, not by a vendor — census data refreshes annually, threshold language lists update on statute, and language access plans are filed on schedule whether or not anyone is watching.

02

No data vendor sells it, because building it means joining a census table to a compliance document to a public ad archive — and the vendors who sell contact data sell none of the three.

03

It is dated and checkable. Public ad archives retain seven years of spend ranges, creative and funding entities, so the gap can be cited from the buyer's own record rather than asserted.

04

The final layer — matched community outlets by language, nationally — cannot be reconstructed by a competitor at any price. ACoM owns the directory that produces it.

01

US Census ACS — Tables B16001 & S1601

The true in-language population of any jurisdiction: people who speak a given language at home AND speak English less than 'very well', across ~42 language groups, at state, county, place and PUMA level. Free API. This is the denominator for every email in the plan.

02

Published Medicaid threshold-language lists

The languages a managed care plan is contractually required to serve in each county — in California, any language spoken by 3,000 beneficiaries or 5% of the county beneficiary population, whichever is lower. Published county-by-county by the state. Powers Tier 2, the revenue tier.

03

Language Access Plans (NYC Local Law 30, CA Dymally-Alatorre, county & city plans)

The agency's own language list, its named Language Access Coordinator, and the plan's own review date — three routing fields and a contact, in one free document. Powers Tier 4.

04

Meta public ad archive (social issues, elections & politics)

Funding entity, spend range, impression range, demographic breakdown and the creative itself — which reveals the language it ran in. Health, civil rights and public education campaigns all fall inside this category. Seven-year retention, free API. Powers Tier 1.

05

Award feeds — USAspending, state grant portals, procurement award notices

Grants and contracts whose scope contains multilingual, outreach, public education or community engagement — with a dollar figure and a period of performance. A budget with an end date is a clock. Powers Tier 5.

06

IRS filings — Form 990-PF grant schedules, Form 990 Schedule H

Foundation grantmaking to outreach programmes, and nonprofit hospital community-benefit spending. Identifies funded outreach that never appears in a public procurement portal.

07

Section 1557 Notices of Availability & site language scrape

What the organisation actually publishes in — hreflang tags, language switchers, localised URL paths, and the top-15-language notice every covered health entity must post. The numerator against the census denominator.

08

The ACoM national directory

Matched community newspapers, radio, TV and digital outlets by language and by county, with audience figures. The layer no competitor can rebuild, and the reason every sequence in this plan ends by naming the outlets.

02 — Waterfall

Routing on the strength of a public commitment

The axis cuts across verticals, never along them. A county health department and a Medicaid plan can sit in the same tier, because what sets the tier is the quality of the public evidence — not the sector.

first match wins 
1. IF Political campaign, candidate committee, party organisation, or an advocacy body whose messaging would compromise ACoM's neutrality with its own outlet network -> HARD EXCLUDE — never enters the cascade (Jeopardises the fiscal sponsorship and spends the trust of the outlet network, which is the actual asset. No exceptions, regardless of deal size.)
2. IF Existing ACoM outlet or directory member, current client, client within trailing 24 months, or an account with an open solicitation where contact could create a procurement conflict -> SUPPRESS (Outlets are supply, not demand. And contacting an agency during a live solicitation can disqualify ACoM from bidding on it.)
3. IF Jurisdiction under 5% limited-English-proficient population or fewer than 15,000 LEP residents, or no observable outreach spend and no active grant -> EXCLUDE — below threshold (The arithmetic does not produce a compelling gap, and a weak gap discredits the strong ones. There is also no existing budget line to attach the pitch to.)
4. IF Federal agency under the current spending posture -> PARK — revisit quarterly (EO 13166 was revoked in March 2025 and federal outreach funding is in retreat. Do not burn sending reputation on a channel that is contracting.)
5. IF Running social-issue ads in the public archive right now, in 2 languages or fewer, in a county with 6+ LEP language groups above threshold -> TIER 1 — Proven spend, proven gap (Every element of the pitch comes from the target's own public record: the campaign, the spend range, the languages it ran in, the languages it missed. Highest intent in the plan. Hand-worked, no automation.)
6. IF Medicaid managed care plan, D-SNP or Marketplace plan whose county threshold-language list exceeds the languages its own member-facing materials appear in -> TIER 2 — Health plans (The most commercially reliable tier: real budgets that do not depend on grant cycles, predictable annual timing, quality measures that in-language outreach directly moves, and an obligation the 2025 executive order did not touch. This is the revenue engine.)
7. IF PR, public affairs or media-buying firm holding a public-sector communications contract that carries small or diverse business participation goals, with no in-house community media desk -> TIER 3 — Agency subcontract (Shortest cycle in the plan, and the only tier where ACoM already holds a named, dollar-quantified case study of exactly this motion. ACoM arrives as a subcontractor, not a competitor — the firm keeps the client.)
8. IF Agency publishes a language access plan naming both its languages and its coordinator -> TIER 4 — Language Access Coordinator (The document hands over the language list, a named contact, and its own review date. Worked as two personas: the coordinator is the champion, the communications lead holds the budget.)
9. IF Grant or contract award in the last 120 days whose scope contains multilingual, outreach, public education or community engagement -> TIER 5 — Freshly funded (A budget with a period of performance is a clock. The buyer must spend it and must report on it — and ACoM delivers placement documentation that drops straight into a funder report.)
10. IF Any remaining organisation with a serve-everyone mandate in a jurisdiction above the LEP floor -> TIER 6 — Catch-all (School districts, transit agencies, utilities, public health departments, hospital systems, libraries and workforce boards. Routed on census data alone, zero enrichment, unlimited volume. Guarantees 100% TAM coverage.)

06 — Launch

Tier 3 first, Tier 2 second

Launch order deliberately deviates from tier order. Tier 3 is the only tier with a proven, dollar-quantified case study of the exact motion — it proves the thesis before the slow tiers absorb budget.

launch first
01

Close the four launch blockers first. Produce one campaign expressed as reach or cost-per-thousand, write up the CDPH vaccine campaign with numbers, confirm procurement readiness (vendor registration, SAM.gov, insurance certificates, and whether fiscal sponsorship satisfies prime contractors' subcontractor requirements), and run a freshness pass on the outlet directory. The directory IS the product — a stale list turns the lead magnet into a liability.

02

Settle the two open decisions in a single meeting: the FTC reference policy (recommend citing it as ten years rather than as current) and a stated minimum viable buy, without which Tier 6 will fill the pipeline with enquiries that cost more to service than they earn.

03

Stand up infrastructure with separate sending domains per tier group. Never mix government and commercial traffic on one reputation. Open tracking OFF on Tiers 2 and 4 and all .gov sending — public-records exposure plus security gateways that strip or flag pixels. Plain text only, no images, no HTML templates.

04

Build the Language Coverage Gap pipeline: Census ACS API for the denominator, published obligation documents for the commitment, ad archive and site scrape for the numerator, ACoM directory for the close. This one pipeline routes the list, personalises the copy and produces the lead magnet.

05

Launch Tier 3 first at low volume — the only tier with a proven, dollar-quantified case study of the exact motion. Commercial buyers, shortest cycle, and it proves the thesis before the slow tiers absorb budget.

06

Launch Tier 2 second, once the health-sector proof point exists. This is the revenue engine: real budgets, predictable annual cycles, and an obligation the 2025 executive order did not touch.

07

Hand-work Tier 1 continuously in parallel at under 100 accounts. Highest value per account in the plan, no automation, and every claim quoted from the buyer's own public record.

08

Run Tier 5 continuously off award feeds rather than in batches — a period of performance is a clock, and the plan must arrive while it is still running.

09

Open Tiers 4 and 6 once the Reach Gap Report pipeline can serve volume without manual assembly. Publish the 2026 Language Access Field Guide alongside Tier 4 as an ungated sector resource.

10

Run the side motions on the same infrastructure: supply-side reactivation of dormant directory outlets, the alumni motion into pre-2017 New America Media clients, and the HCoM Expo as a sponsorship-sales play.

11

Cap government tiers at 40 sends per mailbox per day. Send Tuesday to Thursday, 09:00 to 11:00 local, avoiding Mondays and any week containing a fiscal-year close.

12

Review weekly against per-tier measures, not one revenue number. Tier 4 is judged on champions created and Tier 2 on qualified meetings over a six-to-twelve month cycle — judging every tier on in-period revenue will kill the good ones.

Scale / cut rules

Tier 1 at 15%+ reply, 6% positive Working as designed. Expand the hand-worked pool, but keep it under 100 live accounts — this tier degrades the moment it is automated.
Tier 2 at 5-8% reply, 2% positive On target. Judge on qualified meetings, never on in-period revenue — the cycle is six to twelve months. Resource this tier above all others once the proof point exists.
Tier 3 at 8-12% reply, 3% positive On target. Two subcontract conversations per month is the real measure. Scale sending volume and widen the award-notice net.
Tier 4 at 6-9% reply, 4% report requests Healthy. Measure champions created, not revenue. Track which coordinators forward the breakdown to their communications lead — that forward is the actual conversion event.
Tier 5 at 7-10% reply, 3% positive On target. Tighten the award feed to under 120 days and shorten time-to-first-touch; the value decays as the period of performance runs down.
Tier 6 at 2-4% reply, 1% positive Expected. This is the volume and coverage tier. If it drops below 2%, the LEP floor is set too low — raise it rather than rewriting copy.
