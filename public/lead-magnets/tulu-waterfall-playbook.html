01 / Signal

The amenity array is the whole programme

Every PBSA and BTR listing in Europe publishes a machine-readable list of what that building offers. That single field routes the waterfall, qualifies the account and supplies the personalisation, all from public data.

strongest signal
01

It is published, structured and free. HousingAnywhere, Uniplaces, Spotahome, Idealista, Funda, SeLoger and Immobiliare all expose an amenity array per property.

02

It maps one-to-one onto TULU's product. Absence of any product-access category is a literal statement of the gap TULU fills.

03

Nobody sells it as a lead list, so it cannot be bought by a competitor and it does not decay like firmographic data.

04

It doubles as the personalisation. The same field that tiers the account supplies the first line of the email, which is why the copy is cheap and accurate.

02 / Routing

First match wins, ordered by proximity to a decision

A 300-bed independent opening in six months beats a 40,000-unit landlord with no trigger, because the first has an open budget line and the second has a closed one. Every account lands in exactly one tier and nothing is discarded.

first match wins 
1. IF The asset is opening, opened within the last 6 months, or is in active lease-up within the next 12 months -> Tier 1 - Lease-Up Window (The amenity spec is not yet frozen, the marketing budget is live, and the only KPI on the wall is speed-to-full. TULU arrives as a leasing accelerator with no CapEx line, not as an operating expense. It is the one tier where the buyer is already looking for what we sell.)
2. IF The building already lends, hires out or stocks items using human labour, evidenced on the facilities page, in the resident handbook, in reviews or in a front-desk job advert -> Tier 2 - Manual Workaround (They have already validated resident demand with their own money and are paying staff time to service it badly. There is no concept to sell, only a delivery mechanism. Shortest logical distance from cold email to yes.)
3. IF Operating asset with an amenity array in the bottom third of its own submarket cluster, and no product-access category at all -> Tier 3 - Amenity Gap vs. Competitor Set (The pain is real and quantified but not yet urgent. The email has to manufacture the urgency by showing them a comparison they have never run. Competitive-set framing is the most reliably opened angle in real estate outbound, but it converts below T1 and T2 because no budget event is live.)
4. IF The owner or operator publishes a GRESB, CSRD or EU Taxonomy disclosure with circular-economy, resource-sharing or resident-engagement commitments -> Tier 4 - Circularity and ESG Mandate (Different buyer entirely. This letter goes to Head of ESG, not to operations, and the NOI argument actively weakens it. One vacuum serving 200 apartments instead of 200 vacuums in 200 cupboards is measurable circularity with an audit trail. Slow individually, but it is where portfolio-wide deals come from.)
5. IF Anything else in the six markets above the size floor of 120 beds, 100 units, or 3 or more assets -> Tier 5 - Catch-All: Dead Square Metres (Mandatory. Signal-based routing systematically misses buildings that are simply not very online, and in Southern Europe a large share of good independent operators have a thin digital footprint. Discarding them would be discarding revenue, not disqualifying it. Treat replies here as intelligence for re-tiering.)
6. IF The recipient is domiciled in Germany -> Excluded from email entirely, routed to LinkedIn, Expo Real and referral (Under UWG Section 7, unsolicited commercial email to a German recipient requires prior consent, including B2B. Enforcement is real and penalties reach EUR 300,000 per case. Roughly 500 to 700 contacts are suppressed from the sequenced programme.)
7. IF CROUS, Studierendenwerk, social DUWO and SSH stock, university-controlled Colegios Mayores, assets under the size floor, hostels and serviced apartments, co-living with an all-inclusive equipment model, existing TULU customers and their sister assets, or proptech vendors and brokers -> Excluded before the build (No commercial amenity autonomy, unit economics that do not carry an installation, or a different product entirely. Removing these protects sender reputation and TULU's brand more than any deliverability tactic.)

Lease-up sells speed. Manual workaround sells relief. Competitor gap sells FOMO. ESG sells evidence. The catch-all sells dead square metres. Same product, five genuinely different letters.

03 30-35% TAM

Tier 3: Amenity Gap vs. Competitor Set

Operating asset whose advertised amenity array sits in the bottom third of its own submarket cluster, with no product-access category. Built by pulling every asset in a city, clustering by postcode or campus proximity, and scoring distinct amenity categories.

Pain
They are losing tours and renewals on the small stuff, not on the big stuff. A cinema room gets used four times a year. A vacuum gets used monthly, by everyone. Nobody in the building has ever run the comparison against the street.
Offer
Full submarket amenity comparison, sent whether or not they take a call
Source
Aggregator amenity arrays clustered by submarket, plus resident review text for the quote
Reply density
Moderate. Reliably opened, but no live budget event, so it converts below T1 and T2.
04 10-14% TAM

Tier 4: Circularity and ESG Mandate

The owner or operator publishes a GRESB submission, CSRD report, EU Taxonomy disclosure or annual report containing circular-economy, resource-sharing, waste-reduction or resident-engagement commitments. Xior, Vonovia, LEG, Vesteda, Bouwinvest, Amvest, Catella, Patrizia, Nexity, in'li, CDC Habitat.

Pain
The circular-economy commitment is clear at asset level on materials, energy and waste. Anything resident-facing that can actually be evidenced is thin or missing, and resident-engagement claims go into the submission without a denominator.
Offer
Anonymised portfolio benchmark framed for GRESB resident-engagement and circular-economy reporting
Source
GRESB participant lists and scores, CSRD PDFs, annual reports, INREV and ULI Europe membership
Reply density
Low volume, high value. ESG buyers do not hold amenity budgets and move on annual cycles. Run at low volume with hand-finished copy.
05 25-30% TAM

Tier 5: Catch-All: Dead Square Metres

Everything else in the six markets above the size floor. 120+ beds for PBSA, 100+ units for multifamily, or 3+ assets for a multi-asset operator. No trigger required and none claimed.

Pain
Every one of these buildings has a room that generates nothing. A former business centre, a half-used games room, a corner of the lobby. It costs money and earns nothing, and no one has ever costed it.
Offer
One-page Amenity Gap Report for the named property, no call required
Source
Association directories and national registers, for the operators whose digital footprint is too thin to trigger T1 to T4
Reply density
Lowest per send, highest volume. Its real job is surfacing accounts we mis-routed, so treat replies as re-tiering intelligence.

Days 1-14. Document and sign off the Legitimate Interest Assessment before a single send, and brief native copywriters in Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese. Budget for native rewriting, not translation.

06

07

Days 46-75. Add Portugal, Italy, France and the Netherlands. Open Tier 3 and Tier 5. Run Tier 4 at low volume with hand-finished copy. Move to full send capacity of 450 to 540 per day and review at tier level weekly.

08

Days 76-90. Kill or rewrite any tier below its floor. Re-route accounts whose replies revealed the wrong tier. Feed objections from pilot calls back into email three. Decide whether office and hospitality warrant a parallel programme.

09

Throughout. Open tracking stays off, because pixel tracking is a GDPR exposure in the EU and degrades deliverability. Measure on replies and on gap-report requests only.

Throughout. Respect the academic calendar. September and October are turn-in chaos, November to February is the buying window for the next academic year, March to May is the second window, and August is dead across Southern Europe.

Scale / cut rules

Bounce rate under 2% Healthy. Above 3%, stop sending and rebuild the list before touching the copy.
Tier 1 reply rate 12-18% On target. The live decision window is doing the work. Below 10%, the opening-date data is stale.
Tier 2 reply rate 10-16% On target. Below 8%, the quoted evidence is too generic to be recognisable as their own building.
Tier 3 reply rate 6-10% On target. Below 4%, the submarket clustering is too loose and the comparison is not credible.
Tier 4 reply rate 5-9% On target for low-volume, hand-finished copy. Do not scale volume to compensate.
Tier 5 reply rate 3-6% On target for a volume tier. Its real value is surfacing mis-routed accounts, so read the replies as re-tiering intelligence.
Positive share of replies at 40% or more The tier premise is right. Below 30%, the premise is wrong, not the copy. Fix the routing, not the words.
Gap reports requested by 60% or more of positive replies The primary CTA is working. Below 40%, the offer is not landing as free and specific.
Report to meeting at 35-50% The report is good enough. Below 25%, the report itself is the problem and no amount of email volume fixes it.
Meeting to pilot at 25-40% The risk reversal is doing its job. Below 20%, the 90-day removable pilot is not being led with clearly enough.
Negative sentiment above 0.4% of sends Hard stop. Pause the tier, diagnose, and do not restart until the cause is found. This threshold outranks reply rate.
Tier 1 and Tier 2 not clearly outperforming Tier 3 to Tier 5 The routing signal is not actually being applied. The fix is in the data layer, not in the copy.
