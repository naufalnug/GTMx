Prepared for Anthill Cross Docking

The Anthill Cross Docking outbound journey.

One signal. One cascade. Every company whose freight touches Atlanta without a dock of its own.

Core insight: The freight is public record. Every ocean container entering the US generates a bill of lading released under FOIA, and every broker and carrier in the country sits in a free federal census with its truck count. Join those two files and you can identify — for free, at scale — exactly whose freight touches metro Atlanta and what it costs them not to have a dock here. No data vendor sells that field, so no competitor can buy this list.

The waterfall

One cascade, exclusive routing, 100% coverage

Every account is tested top-to-bottom and routes to the first tier it matches. Nobody is excluded, nobody appears twice.

first match wins 
1. IF Containers discharged at Savannah or Brunswick, delivery address in Georgia, and no warehouse of their own -> Tier 1 — The Demurrage Clock (The box has to be stripped somewhere. That is mechanical, not an opinion — and the per-diem clock is already running.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, and ships into a big-box or grocery DC network with no Georgia inventory position -> Tier 2 — The Chargeback Clock (Must-arrive-by windows are narrow, early is penalised the same as late, and Walmart alone charges 3% of cost of goods per non-compliant case.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, holds active broker authority with zero power units, domiciled in GA, FL, AL, TN, SC or NC -> Tier 3 — The Broker With No Dock (A broker authority with no trucks cannot cross-dock anything itself. The federal record says so.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1, 2 or 3, operates 3 to 100 power units, domiciled in GA, FL, AL, TN, SC or NC -> Tier 4 — The Shifted Load (A carrier with trucks and no warehouse has nowhere to correct a shifted load when the receiver refuses it.)
5. IF Everything remaining in the market -> Tier 5 — No Georgia Node (catch-all) (Selling into the Southeast from an out-of-state node means every regional order is a three-to-five day ground transit, and a lease has never been worth signing to fix it.)

Launch

Phased so every tier gets a clean read

Tier 1 first. Smallest tier, highest intent, only fully automated lead magnet — and it proves the data thesis fastest.

launch first
01

Week 0 — Close the four launch-blocking proof gaps with the client: substantiate or retire the "50% cost reduction" claim, confirm bonded / CFS / customs status, publish dock door count, ceiling height, equipment list and hours, and build the rate card.

02

Week 0 — Pull the Savannah and Brunswick bill-of-lading query and the FMCSA Licensing & Insurance census. Build the Georgia-warehouse disqualifier pass that gates Tiers 1, 2 and 5.

03

Week 1 — Launch Tier 1, capped at 1,500 contacts. Smallest tier, highest intent, strongest lead magnet, and the fastest proof that the data thesis works.

04

Week 2 — Launch Tier 3. Highest lifetime value per reply, because one broker becomes a recurring account rather than a one-off job. Requires the rate card from week 0.

05

Weeks 3 to 4 — Read Tiers 1 and 3 against thresholds, per tier and never in aggregate. Launch Tier 4: largest transactional volume, shortest copy, fastest cash.

06

Weeks 5 to 6 — Launch Tier 2, once the food-grade / FDA / AIB certification question is answered and the staging map is built.

07

Week 7 onward — Launch Tier 5, the catch-all, at whatever volume remains. This tier then runs indefinitely and guarantees complete market coverage.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier immediately.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Deploy the second copy variant, varying only the notice line or the lead magnet.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500 sends Cut the tier and rework the angle.
