01

The signal

Fulfilment and market posture. Every physical-goods brand publicly answers where it ships to and where it ships from. Cross those two and you get an ordered ladder of how far a brand has travelled from garage-packing to multi-node distribution — and exactly where it is stuck.

strongest signal
01

It is free to read. The country selector, the shipping policy, the checkout rate response, the customs record and the job board are all public.

02

It cannot be faked. A brand either ships to the UAE or it does not, and a bill of lading either names a 3PL or names the brand's own dock.

03

It changes slowly. Fulfilment posture moves once or twice a year, so a list built this quarter is still accurate next quarter.

04

It maps one-to-one onto what ShipCube actually sells. Every rung on the ladder has a matching capability, so no tier requires an invented promise.

01

Store Leads / BuiltWith Shopify index

The base universe. Filtered to US, physical goods, estimated revenue above $1M — roughly 1,000 orders a month, ShipCube's practical floor. Also delivers the tech-stack detections that drive Tier 1.

02

Storefront page-source scrape

Homepage plus the shipping and refund policy pages. Reveals cross-border checkout JavaScript (Global-e, Zonos, Passport, ESW, Reach, Flow), the country and currency selector contents, transit-time promises, and any ships-from copy.

03

Shopify shipping-rate probe

A direct rate request against AE, SA, NL, DE and GB. Gives a definitive answer to whether the store ships to a market and what it charges — the exact number that opens the Tier 1 email.

04

US bill-of-lading records (ImportYeti)

The unbuyable one. Consignee and notify party on inbound containers reveal whether freight lands at the brand's own address (self-fulfilling) or at a named 3PL's dock (incumbent identified by name), plus container cadence as a volume proxy. Free, from a US federal disclosure.

05

Job boards

Careers page, LinkedIn Jobs and Indeed, keyword-matched for warehouse associate, fulfilment, pick pack, shipping coordinator, logistics manager and 3PL. Confirms in-house fulfilment and signals scale-up intent.

06

Gulf marketplace reseller sweep

The second unbuyable one. A brand-name search on Noon.com, Amazon.ae, Namshi and Ounass finds third-party sellers listing the brand's SKUs in a market the brand does not serve — public, dated, screenshot-able proof of unserved demand.

07

Social demand sweep

Instagram and TikTok comments on the last 20 posts, keyword-matched for Dubai, UAE, KSA, Saudi, Kuwait, Qatar and ship-to. Produces verbatim customer requests that can be quoted directly in the email.

02

The routing

First match wins. Each brand is tested top to bottom and stops at the first tier it satisfies, so no brand receives two pitches and no brand is left uncovered.

first match wins 
1. IF A cross-border checkout app is detected (Global-e, Zonos, Passport, ESW, Reach, Flow), OR a localized currency or country-specific market is live -> Tier 1 — Committed Globalist (They have already paid to solve duties and checkout. That is proven budget and proven international intent, which makes this the highest-value list in the cascade.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, AND a Gulf or EU reseller is listing their SKUs, OR two or more public customers have asked whether the brand ships to the region -> Tier 2 — Proven Unserved Demand (Demand exists, is dated, and is being monetised by somebody else. This is the sharpest emotional trigger in the ladder because the loss is already happening.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, AND a named 3PL appears as consignee or notify party on their US import records -> Tier 3 — Single-Node Incumbent (Fulfilment is handled, so this is not a do-you-need-a-3PL conversation. The pain is structural: one node, a contract, and no international answer.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1, 2 or 3, AND freight lands at their own address, OR they are hiring warehouse and fulfilment staff -> Tier 4 — Self-Fulfilling & Scaling (They fulfil in-house and it is scaling. Every extra hundred orders a month costs a person rather than a margin point.)
5. IF Everything else — any remaining US physical-goods brand above $1M -> Tier 5 — Catch-All (No visible acute pain. Fulfilment is fine, which usually means unexamined. The catch-all is mandatory: without it the waterfall leaves market on the table.)

The five tiers

Tier 1 is the smallest and richest. The catch-all is the volume engine. Pain is inferred from the trigger in every case, never invented, and every value proposition maps to something ShipCube operates today.

The launch

Tier 2 launches first, ahead of the richer Tier 1. Loss beats capability in cold outbound, and burning a 3,000-account list on unvalidated copy is the expensive mistake.

launch first
01

Weeks 1 to 3 — Launch Tier 2, Proven Unserved Demand, at 1,500 contacts. Not Tier 1. It has the sharpest trigger, the most quotable email, and it validates the reseller-sweep pipeline at the lowest cost. Tier 1 is the higher-value list but it is only ~3,000 accounts and its message is a capability argument, where Tier 2's is a loss argument backed by a screenshot. Loss outperforms capability in cold outbound, and burning the small rich list on unvalidated copy is the expensive mistake.

02

Weeks 3 to 5 — Launch Tier 1, Committed Globalist, at 1,500 contacts, using the cross-border narrative Tier 2 has now proved.

03

Weeks 5 to 8 — Launch Tier 4, Self-Fulfilling & Scaling, at 3,000 contacts. Fastest close and smallest deal size, so it builds early logo count and reply volume while Tier 1 and 2 deals mature.

04

Weeks 8 to 11 — Launch Tier 3, Single-Node Incumbent, at 4,000 contacts. Longest cycle, and it should only start once there are named cross-border references to point at.

05

Week 11 onward — Launch Tier 5, the catch-all, at 6,000+ per month. This is the volume engine and it runs indefinitely on the benchmark magnet.

06

Infrastructure throughout — separate sending domains per tier group, with Tiers 1 and 2 on one and Tiers 3, 4 and 5 on another, so a catch-all volume push can never damage the deliverability of the highest-value tiers. Three mailboxes per domain, 30 to 40 sends per mailbox per day at steady state, two-week warmup before any tier goes live.

07

Before launch — resolve two blockers. Every ShipCube case study is currently anonymous, and there is no named performance reference for the Dubai, Riyadh or Amsterdam nodes, which is exactly what Tiers 1 and 2 rest on. A first name plus category plus metric is enough if full logo rights are not available.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Perfect. Scale the tier hard and add list volume as fast as the pipeline can build it.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched and resist the urge to optimise a working tier.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Ship one copy variant and hold subject-line style, sequence timing and CTA constant so the comparison means something.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate below 1% after 1,500 sends Cut the tier and rework it. Two variants per tier is the maximum before a rebuild.
