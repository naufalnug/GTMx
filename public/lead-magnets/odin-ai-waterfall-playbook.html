02

The routing logic

First match wins, top to bottom. A company hiring an AI engineer while running UiPath routes to Tier 1 only — no account sits in two tiers, and the catch-all guarantees none sits in zero.

first match wins 
1. IF An AI or automation requisition is open (Head of AI, AI Engineer, ML Ops, Intelligent Automation Lead, Agent Engineer) -> Tier 1 — Building It In-House (Budget is approved and the clock has started, but the plan is to hire the capability rather than buy it. This is the sharpest signal in the cascade, so it is tested first.)
2. IF No open AI req, but a legacy RPA vendor is detected (UiPath, Automation Anywhere, Blue Prism, Power Automate) -> Tier 2 — RPA Incumbent (They have already bought automation and already hit its ceiling. The pain is specific and the cost contrast is the hardest in the playbook.)
3. IF No AI req and no RPA vendor, but the account is in a regulated industry AND states a deployment or data-residency constraint -> Tier 3 — Can't Send Data Out (The appetite exists and the blocker is architecture. Odin's on-prem and air-gapped deployment is the only value prop that matters here.)
4. IF None of the above, but three or more manual back-office requisitions are open (claims, AP/AR, contracts, KYC, credentialing, support) -> Tier 4 — Hiring the Headcount (Volume is growing and headcount is the only lever they have. The pain is visible in the careers page and denominated in salary.)
5. IF Everything else in the defined TAM -> Tier 5 — Catch-all: The ROI Benchmark (No visible commitment yet, but the same cost base as their peers and a board asking about AI. Lead with peer benchmark rather than with pain.)

Launch and measurement

Phase-roll the tiers, read nothing before 1,500 sends, and let one number decide whether a tier scales, holds, gets tested or gets cut.

launch first
01

Week 0 — Confirm the compliance list (HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001), confirm Accelerator pricing quoted in copy, and sign off the five lead magnets. Four of these block launch.

02

Week 1 — Build the Tier 1 list against the open-req signal. Target 34,000 contacts across ~7,600 accounts before sending anything.

03

Weeks 1-2 — Launch Tier 1 (Building It In-House) as a chaos test: two deliberately different Day-0 copies, everything else held constant. Sharpest signal, fastest proof.

04

Weeks 3-4 — Launch Tier 2 (RPA Incumbent). The list is pre-qualified by the competitors' own websites and the cost contrast is the hardest in the playbook.

05

Weeks 5-6 — Launch Tier 4 (Hiring the Headcount), the largest non-catch-all tier, with function-matched proof metrics as the variant test.

06

Weeks 7-8 — Launch Tier 3 (Can't Send Data Out). Smallest tier and longest cycle, but the largest deal sizes. Run it once the first three are calibrated.

07

Week 9+ — Turn on the catch-all, once the lead magnets have been validated by real replies from the tiers above.

08

Week 6 and every week after — Read tier performance against the thresholds. Act on nothing before 1,500 sends have landed in that tier.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Excellent. Scale this tier aggressively and expand the source query to grow the list.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched — do not optimise a working tier.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test a single copy variant, rotating the lead magnet first before touching the body.
Worse than 1 per 500, or under 1% reply rate after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the trigger — the signal, not the copy, is usually what is wrong.
