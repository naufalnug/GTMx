The Look Media health system journey.

One waterfall, five tiers, and the cold email copy to acquire hospital-system advertisers for shopping-centre pole banners.

Core insight: A health system's willingness to buy street-level media is not a firmographic — it is visible in the market itself. Either a rival system already owns the parking lots in their service area, or they are already spending on outdoor somewhere, or they just opened something nobody knows about yet. The waterfall routes on which of those three is observably true, in that order, and only falls back to persona when none of them are.

Signal

One observable signal, laddered three ways

Route on evidence of local-awareness spend that is visible from outside the building — a rival's banners, their own billboards, or a facility they just opened. Persona only decides the message when none of those fired.

Waterfall

First match wins — every contact exits into exactly one tier

Tiers 1-3 are account-level. Tier 4 and the catch-all are contact-level, applied only when no account signal fired.

first match wins 
1. IF GATE — facility inside a Look Media DMA, size score in the top two quartiles, and a director+ marketing contact found -> Continue to routing (170+ DMAs covers nearly all metropolitan America, but a rural critical-access hospital fails the gate and there is no budget below the size threshold.)
2. IF A rival health system in the same DMA is a current or past Look Media advertiser -> TIER 1 — Competitor in market (The strongest low-hanging fruit. A visible competitive loss inside their own service area, with the open inventory already mapped.)
3. IF The system itself is a confirmed billboard, transit or other OOH buyer -> TIER 2 — Already buying outdoor (The budget line is proven. The argument narrows to format: 12 faces at parking-lot speeds versus one face at 60mph.)
4. IF A new facility or service line was announced in the last 180 days, or filed as upcoming -> TIER 3 — New site or service line (A new site has zero neighbourhood awareness on day one and a fresh, dated budget attached to it.)
5. IF The contact's LinkedIn bio contains explicit media planning or buying language -> TIER 4 — Confirmed media planner (They own the mix and can evaluate the format on planning terms — coverage, faces, CPM, measurement.)
6. IF Director+ marketing, brand, communications or growth title with no media language in the bio -> CATCH-ALL — Referral ask (They may not own media at all. Assuming a pain here is exactly what made the previous agency's copy read as spam.)
7. IF None of the above -> Excluded (No budget signal, no coverage, or no reachable contact.)
