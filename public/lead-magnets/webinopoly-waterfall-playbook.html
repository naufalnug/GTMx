Prepared for Webinopoly

The Webinopoly outbound journey.

One cascade. Five tiers. A routing signal that is computed, not bought.

Core insight: There are two Webinopolys, and only one of them survives cold outbound. A $50/hour development shop competes with every offshore studio on earth and cannot fund an outbound channel — the allowable CAC is a few hundred dollars. But the agency that tripled a brand's revenue in 90 days while taking it from 34 people to 16 is selling something nobody else in the category sells, at a price outbound economics actually support. This playbook leads with the operating system and treats Shopify projects as the downsell accepted on the call.

The signal

Every storefront broadcasts its own condition, for free

Three public, unauthenticated endpoints — joined per domain — let us compute how much money a specific brand is losing before we ever contact them. The list cannot be bought because it does not exist until somebody computes it.

strongest signal
01

The Chrome UX Report only publishes origin data for sufficiently-trafficked sites — so the same free lookup that diagnoses the pain also qualifies the lead. No revenue estimates, no enrichment vendor.

02

It is real user field data, not a Lighthouse score. These are the prospect's own customers on their own phones in the last 28 days, which is very hard to argue with in a cold email.

03

The signal is computed across three datasets rather than purchased as a database attribute, so no competitor can buy the same list.

04

CrUX refreshes daily on a rolling 28-day window, so a store that passes today can fail next month — the same domain legitimately re-enters the market with a genuinely current finding.

01

/products.json

Free and unauthenticated on effectively every Shopify storefront, returning up to 250 full product objects per request — and it keeps working on headless builds where theme detection fails. Gives catalogue scale, variant complexity, a computed AOV rather than an estimated one, and merchandising velocity from the update timestamps. Separates a real business from a dropshipping husk in a single call.

02

Chrome UX Report API

Google's public dataset of real Chrome user experience — free REST access at origin level, refreshed daily across a rolling 28-day window. Returns mobile LCP, INP and CLS. Doubles as the qualification gate: no CrUX record means not enough traffic to justify the engagement, and the account drops out of the market automatically.

03

Meta Ad Library

Free, public, no login. Returns every currently active ad for any advertiser. Its role is narrow but essential — it proves the brand is actively buying traffic, which turns a performance problem from an abstraction into something costing money by the click.

04

Storefront HTML source

Theme name and age from the Shopify.theme object, installed app footprint, and the third-party script count on product pages — usually most of the explanation for a failing LCP.

05

LinkedIn and job boards

Open requisitions for customer support, operations and ecommerce roles. Presence-based and therefore reliable, unlike inferring absence of a tool.

06

Platform version fingerprint

Magento and Adobe Commerce version, mapped to its published support clock — regular support ending May 2027, extended May 2028, Cloud decommission enforcement June 2028.

The routing

One cascade, first match wins

Priority runs by how forced and how dated the spend is, not by deal size. Every lead exits the matrix with exactly one tier assignment, and nothing in the qualified market falls through.

first match wins 
1. IF Shopify non-Plus AND legacy checkout customisation AND running ads -> Tier 1 — Checkout Breakage (The only tier where the triggering event has already happened on a known date, with damage accruing daily. Shopify auto-upgraded these stores on 26 August 2026 and stripped whatever sat in checkout.liquid and Additional Scripts.)
2. IF ELSE IF 150+ products AND (hiring ops or CS OR 600+ variants) AND 10+ active ads -> Tier 2 — Outgrown the Team (Deliberately placed above Tier 3. A 200-SKU brand with thirty live ads and an open support req would also satisfy Tier 3's condition, but routing it to a speed pitch spends the highest-value opportunity in the book on a fifteen-thousand-dollar project.)
3. IF ELSE IF running ads AND fails Core Web Vitals on mobile -> Tier 3 — Paying to Bounce (The workhorse. Largest reachable volume, fully computable pain, evergreen, and no external dependency — which is why it launches first.)
4. IF ELSE IF platform is Magento, Adobe Commerce, WooCommerce or BigCommerce -> Tier 4 — Platform Refugee (Carries the largest single project value but sits low because the buying window is twelve to twenty-four months out. High value, low urgency.)
5. IF ELSE -> Tier 5 — Catch-All: Stale Storefront (Everything else clearing the qualification gate. Real traffic on a storefront nobody has touched in years — not on fire, just quietly compounding a loss on every session.)

Tier 1: Checkout Breakage

Non-Plus Shopify store with legacy checkout or Additional Scripts customisation, currently running ads.

Pain
Shopify auto-upgraded the store on 26 August 2026 and stripped every legacy checkout customisation. If purchase-event pixels sat in the Additional Scripts field they are gone — and Meta and Google have been optimising on partial conversion data ever since. It throws no error. Spend just quietly gets less efficient, and it usually surfaces six to eight weeks later in a monthly review, after budget has already been reallocated on numbers that were not real.
