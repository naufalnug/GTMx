Prepared for CometChat

The CometChat outbound journey.

One priority-routed waterfall across ~60,000 accounts, built entirely on signals your prospects ship inside their own apps.

Core insight: Chat infrastructure is one of the very few categories of software a company cannot hide. The client SDK ships inside the product and its network endpoints are declared in the app's own code. So the right way to segment this market isn't by vertical — it's by the current state of each company's real-time conversation layer, and how visibly it's straining.

01

The signal nobody can hide

Vertical tells you why a company might want a conversation layer. It tells you nothing about whether they can buy one this quarter. The state of their real-time layer tells you both.

strongest signal
01

The SDK ships inside the product. A decompiled Android bundle or a CSP connect-src header names the incumbent vendor with certainty — no guessing, no vendor database.

02

It's a genuine ladder, not a set of categories. Renting the layer, shipping an agent without a front end, owning a socket layer, leaking conversations off-platform, never having considered it.

03

It costs nothing to refresh. Store crawls, APK grabs, changelogs, job boards and review corpora are all free, so the routing layer doesn't degrade as the list grows.

04

Nobody else is working it. Every CometChat competitor sells primarily into mobile, and web-only detection tools are blind there. The APK grep is an unbuyable list.

02

How every lead gets routed

First match wins. Every account in the market exits with exactly one tier, and the catch-all guarantees nothing is left on the table.

first match wins 
1. IF chat_vendor_detected = cometchat -> Suppress (Existing customer. Never tiered, never sent to.)
2. IF A named third-party chat or RTC vendor is detected in the shipped app -> Tier 1 — Renting the Layer (Budget is proven, the buyer is identified, and a renewal date exists. The strongest commercial signal in the market outranks everything below it.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1, and a user-facing AI agent shipped or was announced in the last 12 months (or an AI engineer req is open on a multi-party product) -> Tier 2 — The Agent Without a Surface (An agent that shipped 90 days ago means money is being spent on conversational surface area right now. Recency of commitment beats depth of commitment — which is why this sits above the homegrown tier.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, and a homegrown real-time stack is detected (Firebase, socket.io, Pusher, Ably, Centrifugo, custom WebSocket), or a real-time infra req is open, or they've blogged about building their own chat -> Tier 3 — Owning the Layer (They already decided chat matters enough to build it. The argument is never whether, only who maintains it.)
5. IF NOT Tier 1 to 3, and 3%+ of the last 12 months of app store reviews mention not being able to reach the other side, or the help centre documents an off-platform workaround -> Tier 4 — The Conversation Is Leaking (The pain is documented publicly by their own users. No proof asset needed — the personalisation is the proof.)
6. IF Everything else in the market with user accounts and more than one party -> Tier 5 — Multi-Party Product, No Layer (The mandatory catch-all. No signal either way, but the structural need is there, and this is where the twelve-month durability of the strategy lives.)

Tier 3: Owning the Layer

NOT Tier 1 or 2, and a homegrown real-time stack detected (Firebase RTDB or Firestore, socket.io, Pusher, Ably, Centrifugo, custom WebSocket), or an open req for real-time and messaging infrastructure, or an engineering blog post about building or scaling their own chat.

Tier 4: The Conversation Is Leaking

The product needs two parties to talk and has no supported way for them to do it, so they're doing it on WhatsApp, SMS and personal email. 06

Sequencing and what good looks like

Tier 1 first for proof, Tier 3 second for volume. The constraint here is sending capacity, not market size — so sequencing is a real decision.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-3 — Launch Tier 1, Renting the Layer. Highest intent, proven budget, cleanest displacement argument, and the vendor-detection line is the most credible first sentence in the strategy. Two proof gaps block it: there's no published migration-time figure behind "moving is a UI swap, not a rebuild", and the competitor price comparison needs a dated, captured rate card before it goes into a client-facing cost model. Close both before send one, or run the contract-framing variant only.

02

Weeks 3-6 — Launch Tier 3, Owning the Layer. The largest sharp tier at roughly 51,000 contacts, and it validates the coding-agent hook, which is the most differentiated thing CometChat owns. Run the chaos test here from day one: maintenance-tax framing against one-command framing.

03

Weeks 6-9 — Launch Tier 4, The Conversation Is Leaking. Needs no new proof asset, because the personalisation is the proof — it's the prospect's own users talking. Slot it in as soon as the review-scrape pipeline is stable.

04

Weeks 9-12 — Launch Tier 2, The Agent Without a Surface. Highest differentiation in the whole strategy, but there's no named agent-platform customer yet, so the proof beat is empty. Launch when there is one, or when CometChat signs off on a stated estimate. Refresh the trigger monthly — it decays fastest of the five.

05

Week 12+ — Switch on the Tier 5 catch-all, then layer the side campaigns. The volume engine goes live once the four sharp tiers have shown which framing travels. The one exception: the vendor EOL and repricing campaign runs opportunistically from week one, whenever a competitor announcement fires.

06

Infrastructure baseline — six to eight sending domains, three mailboxes each, 14-day warmup before first send, 20 to 25 sends per mailbox per day at steady state. Roughly 12,000 to 15,000 sends a month against ~285,000 routed contacts, so list exhaustion is not a risk on any realistic horizon. Sequencing discipline is what determines the outcome.

07

Suppression, non-negotiable — every existing CometChat customer, every account where the detected vendor is CometChat, every free-tier signup, all partner and reseller accounts, and all of Germany (UWG section 7 makes unsolicited B2B email unlawful there without prior consent).

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Perfect. Scale the tier and add contacts before touching the copy.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test the second copy variant. Do not rebuild the tier.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut and rework — the trigger is usually wrong, not the copy.
Tier 1 replies that say "we're locked in until Q2" Count these. A deferred reply with a date is a qualified pipeline entry, and it won't show up in a reply-rate dashboard as a win. Without a separate metric, Tier 1 gets cut for the wrong reason.
Self-serve signups from Tiers 3, 4 and 5 Instrument them. A meaningful share of these prospects will skip the reply and go straight to the free tier. That's real revenue attributable to outbound and it is otherwise invisible.
Negative sentiment above 0.5% of replies Pause the tier regardless of reply rate. Engineers are vocal and public about bad outbound. This applies hardest to Tier 3.
