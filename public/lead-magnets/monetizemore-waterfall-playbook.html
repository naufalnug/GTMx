The signal: ads.txt

The publisher industry's version of a tech-detection database - except it is free, universal, and published by the prospect.

strongest signal
01

It is mandatory. Any publisher selling programmatic inventory must host ads.txt (or app-ads.txt) or buyers cannot verify their supply.

02

It names the incumbent. Competitor seat IDs and MANAGERDOMAIN lines identify a proven payer without any inference.

03

It grades sophistication. One Google line means AdSense-only. Forty DIRECT lines means an in-house ad ops team running their own wrapper.

04

It costs nothing and covers everywhere. One HTTP GET per domain, no vendor, no geography limit, refreshed by the publisher themselves.

02

Routing the market

Five conditions, evaluated top to bottom. Every publisher matches the first one that fits and enters exactly one campaign.

first match wins 
1. IF ads.txt is missing, OR monthly visits are under 500,000, OR visits fell below 400,000 in any of the last three months -> Exclude from the market (At $8-12 session RPM, 500k monthly pageviews is roughly $4-6k a month. The three-month look-back is what enforces the client's 'consistently for three months' rule without asking anyone.)
2. IF ads.txt contains competitor seat IDs (Mediavine, Raptive, Ezoic, Freestar, Playwire, Snigel, Publift, Venatus, Newor) OR a MANAGERDOMAIN declaration -> Tier 1 - Managed-Partner Takedown (They already pay for managed monetization. The decision is made, only the vendor is in question.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1, AND arbitrage tags are detected on site OR paid and social referral share exceeds 35% -> Tier 2 - Clawback and Ban-Risk (They buy traffic, so a meaningful share of it is invalid. Deductions and clawbacks are already happening whether or not they have diagnosed why.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, AND ads.txt has 10 or more SSP lines, AND Prebid or their own Google Ad Manager is detected -> Tier 3 - DIY Header Bidding (They built the stack themselves. The pain is the maintenance burden and missing buying power, not the absence of a strategy.)
5. IF NOT Tier 1, 2 or 3, AND the file is AdSense-only OR has five or fewer lines -> Tier 4 - AdSense Ceiling (One demand source and no auction pressure. The single largest untapped uplift in the entire market.)
6. IF Everything else that clears the qualification gate -> Tier 5 - Catch-All (The setup is unknown, but at $5k a month or more there is real money on the table and no public benchmark to judge it against.)

Launch and thresholds

Phase-rolled highest-intent first. Re-crawl monthly - tier movement is itself a buying signal.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-2 - Launch Tier 1, Managed-Partner Takedown. Proven payers with the clearest pain, and the fastest proof of the whole thesis.

02

Weeks 3-4 - Launch Tier 2, Clawback and Ban-Risk. Most urgent pain, and the free IVT scan is the strongest magnet in the set.

03

Weeks 5-6 - Launch Tier 4, AdSense Ceiling. Largest volume and the simplest story. This is where scale comes from.

04

Weeks 7-8 - Launch Tier 3, DIY Header Bidding. Longest cycle and biggest accounts, so start it once the shorter cycles are producing.

05

Weeks 9-10 - Launch Tier 5, Catch-All. Backfill the remaining market once the four signal tiers are calibrated.

06

Ongoing - Re-crawl the full ads.txt dataset monthly. Publishers move between tiers as their setup changes, and tier movement is itself a buying signal.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Excellent. Scale this tier hard.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test one copy variant - vary the magnet or the first-line angle, hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Cut the tier and rework it.
Fewer than 1,500 contacts available Do not launch. Below that there is no statistical signal to read.
