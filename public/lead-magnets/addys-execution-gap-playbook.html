One waterfall. Five tiers. 365,000 accounts routed on the size of the bet they have already placed.

The routing

First match wins, and nothing is ever double-sent

Exclusive, priority-ordered, 100% covered. Every trigger is presence-based, so nothing in this cascade depends on proving a negative.

first match wins 
1. IF mac_forward_score = 0, or headcount 500+, or the company is a notetaker or meeting-agent vendor, or a GTM agency -> Suppress. Do not send. (Addys is macOS only today. Burning sends to collect 'we are a Windows shop' replies pollutes reply-rate data at exactly the moment a tier is being judged.)
2. IF agent_platform is not none -> Tier 1 - The Agent Buyer (They have already bought the belief that software should do the work, and budgeted for it. The only argument left is that their agents fire on a webhook and the work starts in a conversation.)
3. IF notetaker_tool is not none -> Tier 2 - The Half-Loop Buyer (They conceded that meetings are lossy at checkout. We only have to sell the second half of the loop, and Addys plugs into their notetaker rather than replacing it.)
4. IF open_req_title is not none and req_posted_days_ago is 45 or less -> Tier 3 - The Human Patch (They have priced dropped follow-through at 90 to 140k a year and are recruiting someone to absorb it. The req usually names the symptom in its own words.)
5. IF booking_link_platform is not none AND exec_stack contains 2 or more of HubSpot, Notion, Linear, Slack -> Tier 4 - The Exposed Calendar (The calendar is publicly open and the destination tools are already wired. Requiring both signals produces one strong tier instead of two mediocre ones.)
6. IF none of the above -> Tier 5 - Catch-All (Everything remaining that is Mac-forward and meeting-heavy. Broadest copy, softest ask, and it keeps the waterfall at 100% coverage.)

The launch

Launch order, thresholds, and the real bottleneck

Tier 1 ships first because it is the untested thesis. Sending is never the constraint. Fulfilling the invite codes same-day is.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-2, Tier 1, 1,500 contacts. Validate the new top-tier thesis first. This is the only genuinely new element in the strategy, so it gets proven or killed before anything else scales.

02

Weeks 2-4, Tier 2, 1,500 contacts. A known-good tier that also acts as a control, giving a clean read on whether 'size of the bet already placed' beats 'proximity to the problem'.

03

Weeks 4-6, Tier 3, 1,500 contacts. The highest personalisation cost in the strategy, so it launches only once fulfilment has been proven at lower volume.

04

Weeks 6-9, Tier 4, 3,000 contacts. The first real volume tier.

05

Weeks 9-12, Tier 5, 5,000+ contacts. Scale only after invite-code fulfilment is demonstrably same-day at volume.

06

Throughout: cap daily sending at whatever can be fulfilled the same day. A promised invite code has roughly a two-hour shelf life, and a prospect who replies and hears nothing until tomorrow is worse off than one never emailed.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier hard.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test one copy variant and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate below 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework it.
Mac-disqualification replies above 5% of all replies Stop the list build and retune the Mac-forward score. The tier's numbers cannot be trusted until this clears.
Invite-code fulfilment latency above 4 hours Pause sending until it clears. This threshold outranks any pipeline target.
