02

The routing

Five tiers, tested top to bottom. Each account lands in the first tier it matches, and the catch-all guarantees nothing in the market is left unaddressed.

first match wins 
1. IF competitor_tool_detected is not none -> Tier 1 — Per-Seat Payer (A partner-directory listing, a case-study mention or a tool-naming job post is confirmation they are already paying by the login.)
2. IF ELSE IF open_social_role is true, posted within the last 60 days -> Tier 2 — Seat Count About to Grow (Hiring is the single moment when licence cost and onboarding friction spike at the same time.)
3. IF ELSE IF client_brand_count is 5 or more -> Tier 3 — Multi-Calendar Operator (Five published client brands means five separate approval loops every week. The pain here is volume, not price.)
4. IF ELSE IF org_type is agency AND headcount is under 20 -> Tier 4 — Boutique (Small enough that the spreadsheet technically still works, large enough that it has started to hurt.)
5. IF ELSE -> Tier 5 — Catch-All: In-House Multi-Brand and Remainder (In-house teams approve across departments rather than clients, and none of those approvers will ever log into a marketing tool.)

The launch

Phase-rolled from strongest signal to weakest, so deliverability is spent proving the seat-math angle before it is spent on the long tail.

launch first
01

Weeks 1 to 3 — Tier 1 only. Build the partner-directory scrape across Sprout, Hootsuite, Agorapulse and Later. Target 3,000 contacts at 1,000 per week, and prove the seat-math angle before anything else is built.

02

Weeks 3 to 6 — Add Tier 2. Stand up the weekly job-post scrape and leave it running permanently. This is the self-replenishing engine that carries the strategy long term.

03

Weeks 6 to 10 — Add Tier 3. This requires the client-roster scrape, the most engineering-heavy component, so build it while Tiers 1 and 2 are already producing replies.

04

Weeks 10 onward — Add Tiers 4 and 5. Highest volume, lowest intent. Only switch these on once sending infrastructure is proven at Tier 1 to 3 volume, so deliverability isn't spent on the weakest lists.

05

Before Phase 1 — Confirm permission to name the agency behind the 60-hour, $4,000 result, confirm whether Cisco and WELLA can be named in cold copy, and confirm the 40 percent multi-calendar discount can be quoted in outbound.

06

Before Phase 1 — Build the five magnets (Seat Math Teardown, Ramp Kit, Approval Blueprint, Approval SOP, Friction Audit) and warm three or more domains across nine or more mailboxes for 14 days.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Excellent. Scale the tier hard and add list volume.
1 per 300 Good. Leave it running untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test one copy variant — swap the lead magnet first and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or under 1% reply rate after 1,500 sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework it.
Two variants per tier, maximum Vary the lead magnet or the first-line research angle, never both at once, or the result can't be attributed.
