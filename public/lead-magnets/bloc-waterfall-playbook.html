Prepared for Bloc

The Bloc outbound journey.

One cascade that reads the public record and knows which of Bloc's five answers an organisation needs — before the first sentence is written.

Core insight: Space is one of the very few things a business buys where the trigger event is a matter of public record before the buyer starts shopping. Planning consents, demolition approvals, land listings, incorporation filings and government tenders all publish, for free, the moment an organisation's relationship to physical space is about to change. Bloc does not need to guess who needs space. It can read it.

01

The signal nobody can buy

Every competitor is emailing the same generic London SME list. The join that powers this cascade — occupier multiplied by consented redevelopment — does not exist as a purchasable field from any vendor, and costs nothing to produce.

strongest signal
01

It is filed and dated. Planning consents, demolition approvals and tender notices carry a public reference number and a decision date, so every email has a verifiable reason to exist.

02

It cannot be purchased. No vendor sells the field 'this business occupies an address that just received demolition consent'. Apollo cannot, Clay cannot, ZoomInfo cannot.

03

It costs nothing. The GLA Planning London Datahub covers all 35 London boroughs on an open JSON API, and Companies House is a free API. Routing 76,000 accounts costs GBP 0.

04

It replenishes itself. Councils publish planning decisions every week, permanently, so three of the six tiers refill without anyone rebuilding the strategy.

01

Planning London Datahub (GLA)

Live feed of every development proposal across all 35 London planning authorities: applicant, agent, site address, description, decision and dates. Free open dataset with a guest-read JSON API. This is the unlock.

02

Local authority planning portals

The same fields outside London via weekly published lists, needed for Home Counties and national modular demand. Free, per council.

03

Companies House API

Registered office address, SIC code, incorporation date, officers, filing history and company type. Supplies both the Tier 4 trigger and the PECR compliance gate. Free.

04

Contracts Finder + Find a Tender

UK public-sector notices for temporary accommodation, welfare units, modular classrooms, site cabins and emergency housing. Free.

05

Rightmove Commercial, Novaloca, Realla, EG

Yards, open-storage land and development plots, with listing age and the owner or agent attached. Free listing data.

06

MHCLG statutory homelessness tables

Which local authorities have rising temporary-accommodation placements and a budget problem to go with them. Free.

07

Google Maps + site scrape

Confirms an occupier is physically at an address and calculates its distance from the four Bloc sites. Free.

02

How a lead is routed

A compliance gate runs first, then six conditions are tested top to bottom. Each account routes to the first tier it matches. Nobody appears twice, nobody is left out.

first match wins 
1. IF Company type is a sole trader or unincorporated partnership -> EXCLUDE from email entirely — route to LinkedIn, direct mail or paid (Under PECR these are individual subscribers requiring prior consent. Limited companies, LLPs, PLCs and public bodies are corporate subscribers and may be emailed for B2B marketing with a working opt-out. Companies House supplies this field natively, so the gate is free — and it matters most in Tier 4, where home-registered businesses are exactly the population most likely to be unincorporated.)
2. IF Approved planning consent in the last 12 months for a temporary building, modular unit, container or welfare unit -> Tier 1 — Consented Modular Need (They have permission and a deadline. They are collecting quotes right now, and the default route offered to them is a hire agreement on a long minimum term.)
3. IF ELSE IF a yard, open-storage plot, development site or car park has been publicly listed for more than 90 days without letting -> Tier 2 — Land Doing Nothing (The land is not letting as land. It earns close to nothing while it waits for a tenant who may never arrive. This is the highest-ACV tier in the cascade.)
4. IF ELSE IF the account occupies an address with approved demolition or change-of-use consent, within 12 miles of a Bloc site -> Tier 3 — Displaced by Redevelopment (The building is coming down. There is a hard move-out date and months of unpriced uncertainty about where the workshop and the stock go. This is the join no competitor can buy.)
5. IF ELSE IF a limited company with a residential registered office, a maker / creative / e-commerce / food SIC, incorporated 12 to 48 months ago, within 6 miles of a Bloc site -> Tier 4 — Outgrowing the Spare Room (The business has outgrown the home it was started in. Stock, kit and mess have become a domestic problem, and every proper studio wants a deposit and three years.)
6. IF ELSE IF a live tender for temporary accommodation, welfare units, modular classrooms, emergency housing or site accommodation — or a public body with rising temporary-accommodation placements — or an international-relief charity -> Tier 5 — Public & Humanitarian Buyers (The requirement is urgent, the delivery window is short, and conventional build cannot meet it.)
7. IF ELSE — every remaining limited company inside the M25 with a space-consuming SIC code -> Catch-all — London Space Consumers (No filed event, but space cost is a permanent London problem and almost nobody re-tests the rate they signed on. 'We will beat any quote' is Bloc's strongest existing claim and needs no research beat at all.)

The campaign set

Six waterfall campaigns plus four time-boxed side plays that sit alongside the cascade and run only once it is stable.

volume + side quests
Focused Focused

Consent-to-Quote Intercept

Intercepts the buyer in the two-week window between consent and quote collection, when the only options they have been shown are hire agreements. Bloc manufactures, so it competes on ownership economics rather than on weekly rate.

Filter
Approved planning consent, last 12 months, application type contains temporary building / modular / container / welfare unit. Contact = named applicant or agent on the filing.
AI strategy
Parse the consent description to extract the unit type, dimensions and intended use, then generate a buy-versus-hire breakeven at that exact specification. The email quotes the planning reference and decision month, which is unforgeable by a competitor working from a bought list.
Value
Own the asset instead of renting it
Niche Niche

Idle Land Yield Model

The highest-ACV play in the programme. Bloc is one of very few operators that can credibly pitch this, because it has designed, built AND managed exactly this kind of scheme across four of its own sites since 2013. Manufacturers do not operate; operators do not manufacture.

Filter
Yard, open storage, development plot or car park listed 90+ days on Rightmove Commercial, Novaloca, Realla or EG. Contact = landowner director or named letting agent, plus asset or estates manager.
AI strategy
Compute what the plot would earn converted, using Bloc's published rate card and the plot's stated square footage, against the ground rent it earns today. The artifact is a one-page yield model addressed to that specific site.
Value
Turn dead ground into rent per square foot
Focused Focused

Consented Demolition Relocation

The sharpest trigger available to any London space operator, and structurally unavailable to competitors. Tone discipline is mandatory — being displaced by a developer is stressful, so the email is a helpful neighbour and never an opportunist. No urgency language, no countdowns.

Filter
Approved demolition or change-of-use consent, joined to businesses currently registered or physically trading at that address, within 12 miles of a Bloc site.
AI strategy
The join is the whole campaign: planning consent gives the address and the date, Companies House and Maps give the occupiers. Generate a relocation options sheet listing every Bloc unit that fits their footprint, priced, with drive distance from their current address.
Value
One move, not two
Broad Broad

Home-Registered Maker Graduation

The volume engine of the cascade at ~25,000 accounts. The PECR gate is load-bearing here — home-registered businesses are the population most likely to be sole traders, who cannot be emailed without consent, so company_type must be enforced before any send.

Filter
Ltd company, registered office matches a residential address, SIC in the maker / creative / e-commerce / food-production set, incorporated 12 to 48 months ago, within 6 miles of Hackney Wick or Woolwich.
AI strategy
Match the SIC code to the craft noun and the nearest site, then price 200 sq ft against what a self-storage chain charges for a fraction of the room. Concrete beats abstract: the artifact shows what GBP 100 a week physically looks like.
Value
Get the business out of the house
Niche Niche

Rapid-Deploy Capability Note

Longest cycle and largest contracts in the programme. Currently gated on proof: Tier 5 sells a humanitarian capability with no public evidence it has been exercised, and no published lead time. Both are fixable with a single client input.

Filter
Live or recent notices on Contracts Finder and Find a Tender for temporary accommodation, welfare units, modular classrooms, emergency housing or site accommodation; plus councils with a rising temporary-accommodation placement trend.
AI strategy
Reference the published notice, then offer a capability pack for the file rather than a response to the competition. Procurement-safe by construction: it commits the buyer to nothing and positions Bloc ahead of the next requirement rather than inside the current one.
Value
Deliverable when conventional build is not
Broad Broad

Beat Your Quote

Attacks the self-storage ratchet: most London storage is sold on a promotional rate that steps up at month four and again at month twelve, so the number the customer agreed is rarely the number they are paying. Bloc's published rate does not do that.

Filter
Every remaining Ltd company inside the M25 with a space-consuming SIC. Contact = operations, facilities or office manager, with founder as secondary.
AI strategy
No research beat required. Lead with distance to the nearest two sites and the guarantee, then ask for one number — the current monthly figure and square footage. The reply IS the discovery call, and answering it honestly (including telling people to stay put) is what makes the campaign credible.
Value
Stop overpaying for the same square footage

Launch sequence

Sequenced by what can be proven today, not by intent strength. Tier 3 launches first because it needs nothing Bloc does not already have on its website.

launch first
01

Week 0 — Prerequisites. Planning London Datahub pull running clean across a 500-record sample with the occupier join confirmed end to end. Companies House company_type wired into the routing pass so the PECR gate is enforced before any send. Reply-handling procedure live: who sends the relocation options sheet, in what format, and what happens once it lands. Sending infrastructure warmed to 2,000 a week with opt-out and sender identity verified on every template.

02

Weeks 1-3 — Tier 3, Displaced by Redevelopment, capped at 1,500 contacts. Sharpest trigger, hardest deadline, and it sells rentable space at prices already published, so it needs no new proof. This is the fastest possible read on whether planning-data routing produces replies at all.

03

Weeks 3-6 — Tier 4, Outgrowing the Spare Room, 2,000 contacts. The largest tier that can launch without closing a proof gap. Volume validation, and the first real test of the PECR gate at scale.

04

Weeks 6-9 — Catch-all, London Space Consumers, 2,500 contacts. 'We will beat any quote' is Bloc's strongest existing claim and needs no research beat, so this tier proves whether volume alone converts.

05

Weeks 9-12 — Tier 1, Consented Modular Need, 1,500 contacts. Launches only once proof gaps 1 and 3 are closed: one named build and a published delivery lead time. Highest intent in the cascade.

06

Weeks 12-16 — Tier 2, Land Doing Nothing, 1,500 contacts. Launches once proof gap 5 is closed with one named commercial scheme. Highest ACV in the programme and worth waiting to do properly.

07

Week 16+ — Tier 5, Public & Humanitarian, 1,500 contacts. Launches once gaps 3 and 4 are closed. Longest cycle, largest contracts, and the tier most damaged by launching without evidence.

08

Proof gaps to close in parallel, in priority order: (1) one named client or case study, (2) an occupancy or tenant-retention figure for the four London sites, (3) a published delivery lead time for Bloc Spaces units, (4) one humanitarian deployment reference, (5) one completed commercial scheme named. Items 1 to 5 are launch blockers for Tiers 1, 2 and 5. Tiers 3, 4 and the catch-all need none of them.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and increase its share of weekly volume.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test exactly one copy variant — vary the lead magnet or the notice-line research angle and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500 sends Cut and rework the tier. Do not judge any tier on fewer than 1,500 contacts sent.
Tier 2 only — 1 per 800 Still viable. The tiers do not share an average deal size, so they should not share a bar. A Tier 4 reply is worth GBP 100 a week; a Tier 2 reply is worth a designed, built and operated scheme. Judge Tier 2 on pipeline value, not reply ratio.
