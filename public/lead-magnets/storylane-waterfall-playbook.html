02

The routing

First match wins. Each account lands in exactly one tier, and the catch-all guarantees nothing is left out.

first match wins 
1. IF A competitor is named publicly, or a competitor embed is detected in the rendered DOM -> Tier 1 - Confirmed competitor payer (The category is already bought and the budget already approved. The only honest conversation left is scope, seat cost and re-capture time.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, AND paid ads are live, AND the destination page's only action is a demo-request form, AND no interactive surface is present -> Tier 2 - Paying for traffic into a locked door (They are paying per click to send a stranger to a form. The pain is arithmetic, not opinion, and both halves are directly observed.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, AND at least one open presales role, OR a product marketing role with demo responsibilities in the description -> Tier 3 - Buying headcount to solve a software problem (They are about to spend $150k to $200k loaded on a person whose first six months is rebuilding the same demo. They wrote the pain themselves in the job description.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1, 2 or 3, AND a free-trial or signup CTA is present, AND no interactive product surface exists before signup -> Tier 4 - Trial-led, product hidden until signup (The trial CTA is observed present and nothing precedes it. Trials start cold, and the trial itself is carrying the whole job of explaining the product.)
5. IF Everything else with a demo CTA -> Tier 5 - Catch-all (The product sits behind a booked call. Weakest signal in the strategy, so it gets the softest CTA and the most generous offer.)

Launch plan

Hottest tier first, largest tier last. Rendered crawling is the critical path before anything ships.

launch first
01

Scrape all eight competitor customer and case study pages - Navattic, Walnut, Reprise, Demostack, Arcade, Tourial, Consensus, Supademo - to seed the Tier 1 list from confirmed payers.

02

Stand up the rendered crawler using Playwright or a JS-rendering scrape. This is the critical path. We tested raw HTTP fetch against twelve live SaaS demo pages and it detected zero embeds, because these tools inject client-side. A raw-fetch pipeline would silently dump every confirmed competitor payer into the catch-all.

03

Validate demo-posture classification against a hand-labelled sample of 200 accounts before trusting it across 78,000.

04

Build the Tier 1 magnet workflow and resource it. At target conversion that is roughly 20 to 50 demo rebuilds a month, and the offer breaks on delivery if nobody owns it.

05

Weeks 1-2: launch Tier 1. Smallest list, hottest intent, fastest proof. 7,400 contacts is enough to read within two weeks.

06

Weeks 3-4: launch Tier 3. Job postings replenish monthly, so the list renews itself without new sourcing work.

07

Weeks 5-6: launch Tier 2, once the ad-library pipeline is built and quality-checked.

08

Weeks 7-8: launch Tier 4, once rendered-crawl accuracy has been proven across the earlier phases.

09

Week 9 onwards: open Tier 5 at volume, only after sending infrastructure is proven on the tiers above.

10

Standing side campaigns, off the waterfall: RepX expansion into existing customers, conference leave-behinds timed to SaaStr and INBOUND exhibitor lists, new-in-seat PMM and presales triggers, and competitor review responders scoring three stars or below.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and widen the source.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test one copy variant and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework it.
