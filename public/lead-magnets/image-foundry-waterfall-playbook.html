The signal

The unbuyable unlock: the prospect's own gallery

No data vendor sells "this scheme launches in March and the gallery has a location map on it." It exists only in the prospect's own public pages.

strongest signal
01

It is free and public. The gallery, the product pages, the job ads and the page source are all open — zero paid enrichment establishes tier membership.

02

It cuts across property and product rather than along them. A housebuilder with a location map in the gallery has more in common with a bathroom brand shipping CAD line drawings than with a solved peer in its own sector.

03

It cannot be bought. Apollo does not know how many images are on a development page. BuiltWith sees a configurator script but not that it covers 4% of the catalogue.

04

The same crawl that routes the lead generates the lead magnet. One pass produces the tier assignment and the personalised asset offered in email one.

The routing

One cascade, tested top to bottom, first match wins

A developer both launching a scheme with no imagery and hiring a 3D artist routes to Tier 1 only. The launch deadline is the hotter signal.

first match wins 
1. IF GATE: entity_class in approved set AND (scale_metric >= 25 SKUs for product, or >= 1 scheme in pipeline for property) AND geo_email_permitted = TRUE -> Continue to Tier 1. If any condition fails, drop from TAM entirely. (Excludes estate agents (the house already exists), architecture practices (referral channel), competing CGI studios, sub-25-SKU brands, Germany and Austria (UWG §7), and UK sole traders (PECR treats them as individuals).)
2. IF (launch_status in {coming-soon, launching-dated} AND launch_asset_state in {none, map-or-siteplan, site-photo}) OR (new_range_flag = TRUE AND new_range_asset_state in {cad-only, cutout-only}) -> Tier 1 — Launch With Nothing To Show (Capital and a public launch date are committed to something that physically cannot be photographed. This is the hottest signal in the cascade because it carries a fixed deadline.)
3. IF ELSE IF viz_job_ad != none AND viz_job_posted_days <= 45 -> Tier 2 — Hiring The Problem (Budget is approved and the workload already exceeds capacity. The job ad also names the hiring manager, so the contact comes free with the signal.)
4. IF ELSE IF threed_stack_detected != none AND threed_sku_coverage_pct < 0.35 -> Tier 3 — 3D Stack, Thin Library (They bought the tool and believe in 3D. The platform has outrun the asset library, so most of the catalogue still cannot be configured. Highest deal value in the cascade.)
5. IF ELSE IF launch_status = now-selling AND (assets_per_development < 8 OR interiors_furnished = FALSE OR 'people' not in asset_mix OR cgi_reuse_detected = TRUE) -> Tier 4 — Selling Live On Thin Visuals (They already pay for CGI and believe in it. They are under-served by their current supplier — flat, cold, thin, recycled. This is a displacement conversation, not an education one.)
6. IF ELSE (everything remaining that passed the gate) -> Tier 5 — Catch-all: The Catalogue Reality Gap (Roomset photography does not scale. Every new colourway and configuration needs another studio day, so the catalogue silently outgrows the imagery. No account can exit the matrix unrouted.)

The tiers

Five tiers, ordered by how much money is already committed

Tiers 1 and 2 regenerate themselves every month. The waterfall recirculates rather than depletes.

A scheme listed Coming Soon or with a dated launch and no CGI in the gallery — or a new product range whose pages carry CAD-or-cutout-only imagery.

Pain

/developments status + gallery classification; product-page image classification
Reply density
Highest in the cascade — a fixed launch deadline the prospect cannot move
02 4% TAM

Tier 2: Hiring The Problem

A live job ad posted within 45 days for a 3D Visualiser, CGI Artist, Archviz Artist, Visualisation Manager, Studio Manager or Content Producer.

Pain
Budget is approved and the workload already exceeds capacity. One hire is a three-month ramp, one skillset and one holiday calendar — and the work is already stacked up behind them.
Offer
Make-vs-Buy Model — fully loaded cost of the role they posted vs. equivalent studio output in year one
Source
LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, company /careers page — including the named hiring manager
Reply density
Very high — approved budget, a named contact, and an argument that is useful even if they hire
03 9% TAM

Tier 3: 3D Stack, Thin Library

A configurator, AR or 3D viewer detected in the page source (Threekit, Emersya, Cylindo, Sketchfab, Matterport, Roomle, model-viewer) covering under 35% of live SKUs.

Pain
They bought the tool and believe in 3D. The platform has outrun the asset library, so a configurator covering a third of the range mostly teaches customers which products are not configurable.
Offer
3D Coverage Map — which SKUs and room types the existing tool actually covers today
Source
BuiltWith + raw HTML/asset scan, cross-referenced against the product crawl
Reply density
Strong — the belief is already bought, so no category education is needed. Highest deal sizes.
04 22% TAM

Tier 4: Selling Live On Thin Visuals

A Now Selling scheme carrying fewer than 8 assets, unfurnished interiors, no people in frame, or CGI reused across two or more schemes (caught by perceptual hash).

Pain
They already pay for CGI and believe in it — they are simply under-served. The current supplier renders what it is sent rather than telling them what should be in the shot, so the gallery is flat, cold, thin and sometimes recycled.
Offer
Listing Image Gap Report — their asset count and mix vs. the 20 fastest-reserving comparable schemes
Source
Gallery count + asset classification + pHash reuse detection across the portfolio
Reply density
Good — a displacement conversation with an existing budget line already approved
05 58% TAM

Tier 5: Catch-all: The Catalogue Reality Gap

Everything else that passes the qualification gate — routed on the computed roomset ratio (lifestyle roomset images ÷ live SKUs), brochure vintage and trade-show exhibitor status.

Pain
Roomset photography does not scale. Every new colourway, finish and configuration needs another studio day, so the catalogue silently outgrows the imagery — 640 products and 11 lifestyle images is not a decision, it is a constraint.
Offer
Roomset Ratio Audit — their computed ratio, the category benchmark, and what closing the gap looks like
Source
Product crawl + brochure vintage + exhibitor directories
Reply density
Broadest and softest — expected to land between 1 in 400 and 1 in 500, which is normal for a catch-all

The Empty Gallery (property)

The hottest tier in the cascade because the deadline belongs to the prospect, not to us. They cannot take off-plan reservations on a location map, and the launch date is already public.

Filter
Developer /developments page shows a scheme as Coming Soon or with a dated launch, and the gallery contains only a location map, site plan or hoarding photo.
AI strategy
Crawl every scheme page in the portfolio, classify each gallery image, and compute the Launch Asset Deficit against the median of the 20 fastest-reserving comparable schemes in the same region and price band. The email names the scheme and quotes the deficit.

The Half-Built Configurator

The blocker is never the platform — it is that somebody has to model and dress every remaining SKU, and that queue never gets to the front of anyone's week. Highest deal value in the cascade.

A 3D/AR/configurator platform detected in the page source, live on under 35% of the brand's SKUs.
AI strategy
Detect the platform via BuiltWith plus a raw asset scan for .glb/.gltf and model-viewer, then crawl the catalogue to compute exactly what percentage of SKUs the tool actually reaches. The email quotes the coverage percentage back to them.

Crawl the sitemap, classify every product image, and compute the ratio. The subject line is two numbers: their SKU count and their roomset count. The body explains that this is a constraint, not a decision.

The launch

Phase the hottest tier first, then add volume

A single reply from a housebuilder eight weeks out from launch is worth more than 5,000 catch-all sends.

launch first
01

Weeks 1–2 — Tier 1 only. Build the property crawler first (/developments status plus gallery classification) covering UK developers and registered providers, roughly 2,900 contacts. Tier 1 launches first because it has the hardest deadline, the clearest pain and the shortest path to a referenceable win.

02

Weeks 3–4 — Add Tier 2. Job-ad monitoring across both universes. Small volume, very high intent, and it seeds the make-vs-buy asset that Tiers 3 and 5 also draw on.

03

Weeks 5–7 — Add Tiers 3 and 4. The product-page crawler and pHash reuse detection come online. Tier 4 carries the biggest property volume; Tier 3 carries the biggest deal sizes.

04

Week 8 onward — Tier 5 at volume. The catch-all opens once the first four tiers have proven copy and the reply-handling process holds. Extend to US accounts here, not before — establish the UK proof first.

05

Infrastructure — 4–6 secondary domains (never the primary imagefoundry.co.uk), 3 mailboxes each, full SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warmed 21 days before the first send. 20–25 sends per mailbox per day at steady state.

06

Compliance — UK PECR: corporate subscribers only with a working opt-out. US CAN-SPAM: opt-out plus postal address. Germany and Austria excluded from cold email entirely under UWG §7; route those accounts to LinkedIn and paid.

07

Ongoing — Re-scan Tier 1 monthly and Tier 2 weekly (both are rolling tiers that regenerate). Tiers 3 and 4 quarterly. Side campaigns (agency white-label, trade-show windows, new marketing leaders, retailer imagery mismatch) open only after the waterfall has run eight consecutive weeks without a copy change.

08

The one thing that breaks it — every email offers a specific personalised asset for one character of effort. If those assets go out late or generic, the tier collapses regardless of copy quality. The crawler must produce the asset automatically, and a named person must own sending it within one working day.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier hard — increase daily volume and widen the geography.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450–500 Borderline. Test one copy variant — change the lead magnet or the opening observation, and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework it.
Under 1,500 sends on any tier Do not read it. Below that threshold the numbers are noise.
