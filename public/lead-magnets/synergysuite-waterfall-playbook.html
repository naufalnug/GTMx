Franchise Disclosure Documents publish the operator's mandated back-office system, their unit growth, and often their food and labour cost percentages — free, annually, by law.

How every account routes

One question, tested top-down: what does this operator's own public record say about their back of house? First match wins. Nobody is excluded.

first match wins 
1. IF unit_count >= 8 AND NOT existing customer AND NOT restaurant-tech or distribution peer AND NOT ghost-kitchen-only -> QUALIFY into the waterfall (Below eight units variance isn't visible and the per-location price floor doesn't clear. Peers and ghost kitchens have no count cycle to manage.)
2. IF brand_count >= 2 OR acquisition_date_18mo IS NOT NULL -> Tier 1 — The Consolidation Clock (A dated event beats an evergreen message. A multi-brand operator with a competitor mandate is better messaged on consolidation than on displacement, so this tier sits above Tier 2.)
3. IF boh_vendor_mandated != "none" -> Tier 2 — The Incumbent On File (Item 11 names the incumbent outright. The email opens with a fact about their own filing that no other vendor has ever quoted at them.)
4. IF net_new_units_24mo >= 8 OR cost_line_vs_benchmark >= +1.5 points -> Tier 3 — The Mandate Gap (flagship) (No mandated system plus either fast growth or a published cost line above median. The pain is arithmetic on their own numbers, not a claim about ours.)
5. IF repeat_critical_violations_12mo >= 3 -> Tier 4 — The Pattern In The Record (Three or more units repeating critical violations means paper logs and no cross-unit visibility. This tier also reaches corporate chains that file no FDD at all.)
6. IF everything else that qualified -> Tier 5 — Catch-all: Multi-Unit, One Spreadsheet (Eight or more units, a detected POS, and no back-office evidence anywhere. Weakest signal, broadest copy, softest CTA — and it guarantees 100% TAM coverage.)

01

Launched first despite not being the highest-intent tier: it has the sharpest single line ("Item 11 lists {{competitor}}"), the strongest named proof (Quiznos: 4% labour, 2% food), 2,580 accounts sitting ready, and a message that needs no arithmetic to land. Fastest route to a proof point.

02

Weeks 3-6 — Tier 3, The Mandate Gap. The flagship and the biggest tier. Launched second so the FDD extraction pipeline is already proven by Tier 2 before we depend on Items 19 and 20. Both variants (growth trigger and disclosed cost line) run from day one as the tier's chaos test.

03

Weeks 6-9 — Tier 1, The Consolidation Clock. Highest intent per contact but event-gated, so the list refills at roughly 60-90 accounts a month. Runs continuously once opened, topped up as deals close.

04

Weeks 8-11 — Tier 4, The Pattern In The Record. Needs the health-inspection pipeline built county by county. Start with the five highest-density jurisdictions — LA County, King County, FL DBPR, Montgomery County MD and Delaware — then expand.

05

Week 11+ — Tier 5, the catch-all. Largest volume, weakest signal. Opened last, once the four sharper tiers have set the benchmark this one gets measured against.

06

Infrastructure, in parallel from week 0: eight secondary domains, three mailboxes each at 25 sends/day, SPF/DKIM/DMARC on all, 21-day warmup before first send. 600/day sustained, scaling to 2,000/day.

07

Quarterly: re-run the routing matrix. A Tier 5 account that opened nine units becomes Tier 3. A Tier 3 account that finally bought Restaurant365 becomes Tier 2 — a better prospect than before, not a lost one.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and raise volume.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test one copy variant — change the magnet or the first-line angle, hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate below 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the trigger.
Minimum readable sample 1,500 sends before any judgement. Every tier in this cascade clears that on its own.
