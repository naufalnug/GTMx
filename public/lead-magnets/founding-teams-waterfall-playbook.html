Prepared for Founding Teams

Your Founding Teams waterfall journey.

One priority-routed cascade that every early-stage founder in the market flows through, built once and fed leads forever.

That visibility is the whole strategy. The single question the waterfall routes on is: how is this founder currently trying to buy build capacity, and how much has it already cost them?

Signal

The one signal every founder broadcasts

Founders advertise their build problem in public. Co-founder posts, equity-bearing job listings, funding announcements and waitlist pages are all the same signal at different intensities, which is exactly what a waterfall needs.

strongest signal
01

It is publicly observable. A co-founder post, a days-open count, a round amount and a waitlist phrase are all scrapeable facts, not inferences.

02

It is exclusive and ordered. The four signal tiers cannot overlap, and every founder who matches none of them is still in the market under the catch-all.

03

It maps one-to-one onto what Founding Teams sells. Equity-only builders, a $12,000 flat build, and a free plan each answer a different intensity of the same problem.

04

It renews itself. Co-founder posts, aging job reqs and fresh rounds all expire, so the top three tiers refill weekly without any new strategy work.

Founder and co-founder titles at companies of 1 to 10 people, plus post-keyword matches on technical co-founder, looking for a CTO and founding engineer.

The days a technical role has stayed open. Scrape twice, diff the set. A req that is still live after 30 days is a fact the founder cannot argue with.

Pre-seed and angel rounds under $2M closed in the last six months, with stage, date, amount and headcount.

06

Product Hunt Upcoming + BuiltWith + page scrape

Pre-product companies: waitlist language present on the page, and no product route resolving behind it. Positive detection, not an absence signal.

07

Apollo + Clay

The remaining founder-led market plus contact enrichment across every tier.

Routing

First match wins, top to bottom

Every account is tested against the tier conditions in order and routed to the first one it matches. Tiers 2 through 5 each carry an implicit and-not-any-tier-above, so one founder lands in exactly one campaign, always.

first match wins 
1. IF A co-founder search is detected, OR equity is advertised with no salary, OR equity is advertised alongside a salary under $60,000 -> Tier 1 - Already sold on equity (This founder has already accepted that equity is the currency. The only objection left is supply and trust, which is precisely what Founding Teams sells.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, AND a technical role has been open 30 days or more, AND headcount is 15 or fewer -> Tier 2 - The req that won't close (An unfilled requisition is a dated, quantified, self-evident pain. The roadmap has not moved in a month and the founder is already weighing whether to lower the bar.)
3. IF NOT Tiers 1-2, AND a pre-seed or angel round of $2M or less closed in the last six months, AND headcount is 10 or fewer -> Tier 3 - Funded, clock running (Money in the bank started a clock. This is the only segment that reliably clears $12,000, so it is the revenue tier despite sitting third on intent.)
4. IF NOT Tiers 1-3, AND waitlist language is on the page, AND no product route resolves, AND headcount is 3 or fewer -> Tier 4 - Landing page, no product (They committed enough to buy a domain and write the positioning, then stopped. Free is the only offer that moves them, and free is one of the things Founding Teams has.)
5. IF Everything else -> Tier 5 - Catch-all (Every remaining founder-led company of 20 people or fewer in scope geography. 63% of the market, and skipping it would break the waterfall's coverage property.)

Tiers

Five tiers, from already-sold to catch-all

Tier 1 founders have already agreed to the business model, so the only objection left is supply and trust. Each tier below is a weaker version of the same signal, and Tier 5 absorbs the rest of the market.

01 4% TAM

Tier 1: Already sold on equity

A public co-founder search on YC Co-Founder Matching or LinkedIn, or a role advertised with equity and little to no cash.

Pain
They have already accepted equity as the currency. The bottleneck is supply and trust - finding, vetting and legally contracting people they have never met. Co-founder searches run six months and most of them die at the equity-split conversation.
Offer
A shortlist of five vetted builders matched to their stack, with backgrounds and availability
Source
Wellfound, YC Co-Founder Matching, LinkedIn posts
Reply density
Highest in the strategy. This is where one positive reply per 150 contacts is genuinely achievable.
02 9% TAM

Tier 2: The req that won't close

A technical role open 30 days or more, headcount 15 or fewer, with a cash salary posted.

Pain
They are paying the cost of an unfilled role. The roadmap has not moved in a month, and the salary budgeted is many times what the outcome is worth at this stage. A first engineering hire at pre-seed is the highest-variance decision a founder makes.
Offer
A cost-of-the-unfilled-req calculation, plus the five-builder shortlist
Source
Wellfound plus a LinkedIn Jobs diff scrape at day 30
Reply density
Strong. The days-open number does the persuading, so the copy can stay short.
03 11% TAM

Tier 3: Funded, clock running

A pre-seed or angel round of $2M or less closed in the last six months, headcount 10 or fewer.

Pain
Money in the bank started a clock. Investors expect a shipped product and traction data before the next round, and every week spent recruiting is a week not spent on customers. A small round cannot absorb a $160,000 founding-engineer salary.
Offer
A free PRD, four-week build scope and UI direction - the three-day planning deliverable, given away
Source
Crunchbase, Tracxn, Dealroom
Reply density
Fewer replies than Tier 1, but this is where the revenue is. Judge it on pipeline value, not reply count.
04 13% TAM

Tier 4: Landing page, no product

Waitlist or coming-soon copy detected on the page, no product route resolving behind it, headcount 3 or fewer.

Pain
They committed enough to buy a domain and write the positioning, then stopped - because the next step costs money they would rather not spend, or a technical co-founder they have not found. Waitlists decay: signups collected today are cold in eight weeks.
Offer
A free six-week MVP scope, written from their own landing-page copy
Source
Product Hunt Upcoming, BuiltWith, on-page scrape
Reply density
Highest raw reply rate and the lowest revenue per reply. Expected, and fine - these founders enter on the free plan.
05 63% TAM

Tier 5: Catch-all

Every remaining founder-led company of 20 people or fewer in scope geography.

Pain
The universal one. Building costs more than an early-stage founder has, in cash or in time.
Offer
The Founder Equity Benchmark: real ranges pulled from 30,000 builders on-platform
Source
Apollo, Clay
Reply density
Lowest per contact, but 63% of the market. Opens last, using whichever value prop the signal tiers proved.

Launch

Tier 1 for replies, Tier 3 for revenue

Both launch in week one so we learn whether the reply problem or the revenue problem is the real one before building the rest. The catch-all opens last, once the signal tiers have taught us which value prop lands.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-2. Launch Tier 1 and Tier 3 together. Tier 1 proves the strategy fastest because those founders already believe in equity-for-labour, and Tier 3 proves the money. Running both from day one tells us whether the reply problem or the revenue problem is the real one.

02

Weeks 3-4. Add Tier 2. It needs the 30-day re-scrape diff to exist, which is why it cannot go first. By week three the second pull is available and days-open is a real number rather than an estimate.

03

Weeks 5-6. Add Tier 4 as a chaos test - two structurally different emails, since there is no prior signal to build on. This is the tier most likely to surprise on reply rate.

04

Week 7 onwards. Open the catch-all. Tier 5 is 63% of the market with no trigger to lean on, so it should only open once the four signal tiers have taught us which value prop lands.

05

Week 10 onwards. Layer the side campaigns: the accelerator-rejection window after each YC, Techstars, Antler and EF batch announcement, and hackathon non-winners via Devpost, MLH and the existing HackerEarth partnership.

06

Before any of it: eight to ten sending domains with three mailboxes each, two weeks of warmup, 20 to 30 sends per mailbox per day, 300 sends daily per active tier, and full verification on every address. Founder lists carry high catch-all and role-account rates.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier - increase daily volume and widen the scrape.
1 per 300 Good. Leave it alone and let it run.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Deploy the second copy variant before changing anything else.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500 sends Cut the tier. Rework the trigger or the magnet before relaunching.
