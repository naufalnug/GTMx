Prepared for Knucklepuck

The Knucklepuck outbound journey.

One cascade. Five tiers. A market that publishes its own budget, its own leadership, and its own buying window — free, every year.

Core insight: Stop offering to look for problems. Show up having already found one. "I could run an audit for you" is the email every competitor sends; "I already ran it, here's what broke, want the list?" is a different email in kind — and it removes the recipient's entire reason to say no.

01

The signal

Every US tax-exempt organisation publishes its revenue, its marketing spend, its leadership roster and its fiscal calendar — annually, by law, in machine-readable form. Nobody in this category reads it.

strongest signal
01

One free API call returns the market, the marketing budget, the decision-maker's name and the buying window — for ~48,000 organisations, at zero data cost.

02

The fiscal year end on a Form 990 tells you exactly when next year's budget gets written. Nobody times outbound to a prospect's budget calendar, because it means reading a tax return.

03

Search problems are the one product surface that is fully public. Broken redirects, stale ad creative, missing bylines — the diagnosis and the pitch are the same artefact.

04

No enrichment vendor sells this join. The data isn't hidden; it's just that nobody in the SEO category has bothered to look at it.

The entire market plus the budget, the dues trend, the officer's name and the fiscal calendar. ~1.8M filings indexed, 40–120 structured fields each, free.

Wayback Machine CDX API

Detects a site relaunch or platform change by diffing CMS and template fingerprints across archived snapshots. This is the Tier 1 trigger.

03

Google Ads Transparency Center

Every live ad an organisation runs, with start dates and formats. Gives creative age and landing-page destination — the Tier 2 trigger.

04

Author bylines, author schema, content library size, CMS generator tags. The Tier 3 trigger.

05

Seed-query search results

Whether an AI Overview cites them or a competitor, and where they place on the searches a prospective member actually runs.

02

The routing logic

Route on how much search revenue an organisation has already put at risk, and how recently. Not industry. Not headcount. Not job title.

first match wins 
1. IF A relaunch or platform change is detected in the last 180 days, OR a staging subdomain is still indexed -> Tier 1 — Migration Risk (Redirect maps ship about 80% complete. The missing 20% is always the deep resource pages holding all the inbound links, and nobody notices for ten weeks. It's the one SEO problem with a clock on it.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1 AND Google Ads are running AND (creative is older than 180 days OR the ad points anywhere other than a dedicated landing page) -> Tier 2 — Paid Waste (Money is leaving the building this month, and you can point at the exact ad in the same free tool they can open themselves. That's not a claim, it's a receipt.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1–2 AND there are 20+ published articles AND (no author bylines OR an AI Overview cites someone else on their core subject) -> Tier 3 — Anonymous Authority (They are the authority in their field by charter, but they publish anonymously — so search and answer engines can't attribute the expertise, and aggregators outrank the source that fed them.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1–3 AND there is an open req for an SEO, digital marketing, content or marketing director role -> Tier 4 — Hiring the Role (Budget is approved and sitting in a headcount line. But one generalist can't cover technical, paid and content, and time-to-fill is ~90 days before day one.)
5. IF Everything else in the market with revenue of $2M or more -> Tier 5 — Catch-all: Member Search Visibility (The dues trend on their own filings is flat or declining. Member acquisition isn't keeping pace, and search is where prospective members start.)

The five tiers

A broken relaunch is bleeding today. A stale ad is bleeding this month. An unattributed content library has been bleeding for years. Urgency descends; copy widens.

Tier 1: Migration Risk

Site relaunched or replatformed in the last 180 days (Wayback CMS/template fingerprint change), or a staging subdomain is still indexed.

Pain
Redirect maps are usually about 80% complete at launch. The missing 20% is almost always the deep resource pages — the ones with every inbound link pointing at them. Nobody notices for roughly ten weeks, because those pages don't get navigated to. They get arrived at.

Highest — acute, dated, and compounding weekly

Tier 2: Paid Waste

Running Google Ads with creative unchanged for 180+ days, or ads pointing at a homepage/generic page rather than a dedicated landing page.

Pain
They're paying for clicks every month and landing them somewhere that asks the visitor to work out where to go next. Six months of static creative means nothing is being tested, so cost per acquisition drifts upward invisibly.
Offer
Landing-Path Teardown — their live ad, their landing page, three named fixes and the cost impact

Reply density

Launch sequence

Tier 2 first, not Tier 1 — because its evidence is a receipt, it needs no pipeline, and it produces the proof metrics the rest of the cascade is missing.

launch first
01

Week 0 — Run the full Form 990 API pass across member-serving NTEE codes at $2M+ revenue. Confirm the ~48,000 estimate before committing any send volume, and bucket every organisation into one of twelve fiscal-calendar cohorts.

02

Week 0 — Close the two launch blockers. Pull a before-and-after CPA figure from any two paid accounts, and get one outcome number from FBA, NAFA, ICGS or NC Bar. Neither Tier 2 nor Tier 5 should send without them.

03

Weeks 1–3 — Launch Tier 2 (Paid Waste). Ads Transparency pull needs no pipeline, the evidence is a receipt the recipient can verify themselves, and it produces revenue proof fastest. Start at 1,500 contacts, scale to 5,300.

04

Weeks 3–6 — Launch Tier 3 (Anonymous Authority). Largest qualified market at ~9,100 and the strongest existing proof. Requires the byline and author-schema scraper.

05

Weeks 6–9 — Launch Tier 1 (Migration Risk) once the Wayback CDX diffing pipeline is built and validated. Highest reply density in the cascade, smallest market at ~2,900.

06

Weeks 9–11 — Launch Tier 4 (Hiring the Role) behind a job-board monitor. ~4,300 organisations.

07

Weeks 11–14 — Launch Tier 5 catch-all, staged at 2,000 per week across ~26,400 organisations, sequenced by fiscal-calendar cohort.

08

Ongoing — Weekly re-scan. Organisations move between tiers as their state changes. An org that sat in the catch-all in March and relaunched in June is not a new prospect; it's an existing record promoted to Tier 1, with a genuine reason to email again.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale this tier hard.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450–500 Borderline. Test one copy variant — rotate the artefact first, not the body.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500 sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the trigger.
