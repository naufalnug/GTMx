01 Signal

The signal: who owns the network under their messaging

Vertical does not predict intent in CPaaS. A healthtech platform sending four million appointment reminders has nothing in common with a twelve-location dental group, but everything in common with a fintech sending four million one-time passcodes. What predicts intent is how deeply a company has committed to programmatic communications, and who currently owns that commitment on their behalf. That is a single ladder-shaped signal, and it is readable for free.

strongest signal
01

The phone number in a company's footer resolves, through a carrier lookup, to whoever provisions their numbers: Twilio, Bandwidth, Telnyx, Vonage, RingCentral or a legacy local carrier. That single field separates a CPaaS customer from a phone-system customer from a company with no program at all.

02

The SMS consent block in a privacy policy or terms page is proof of a live, registered A2P program. Post-2024 carrier consent rules pushed nearly every legitimate sender into publishing the same handful of phrases, and they are trivially detectable.

03

Careers pages and API docs name the stack outright. Twilio, SIP, 10DLC, LiveKit, Vapi or FreeSWITCH in a job requisition is a direct declaration of what a company is running and what it is struggling to staff.

04

All three are free and published by the prospect on their own site, which means routing an account costs nothing. Email discovery is the only variable cost in the pipeline.

02 Routing

First match wins, strictly top to bottom

Each account is tested against the conditions in order and routes to the first tier it matches. A voice-AI startup running on Twilio routes to Tier 2, not Tier 3, because confirmed competitor spend is a harder buying signal than product category. A platform running on Twilio routes to Tier 1, not Tier 2, because the wholesale conversation is a materially bigger contract than a migration.

first match wins 
1. IF The company promises comms features to its own customers AND a competitor CPaaS is detected -> Tier 1 - The Platform Reselling Someone Else's Network (A platform that has made messaging a product feature but does not own the network under it is the largest contract on the board. The wholesale and operations conversation outranks a simple migration, so this test runs first.)
2. IF A competitor CPaaS is detected, or the published number resolves to Twilio, Plivo, Vonage, Sinch, Telnyx or SignalWire -> Tier 2 - Paying for a Ticket Queue (Confirmed, live spend on a direct competitor. This is the hardest buying signal in the market and the fastest tier to prove, which is why it also launches first.)
3. IF The product is an AI voice agent or automated calling system, or an open requisition names SIP, telephony, FreeSWITCH or LiveKit, or the company appears in the FCC Robocall Mitigation Database -> Tier 3 - The AI That Has to Dial (Their product only works if the call connects and gets answered, which makes voice infrastructure a product problem rather than a procurement problem. Small, hot and moving fast.)
4. IF A2P consent language is published, but the number resolves to a business phone system or legacy carrier -> Tier 4 - Texting Without an API (They already text and already registered, the consent block proves it, but they are doing it through a bolt-on with throughput caps and no path to RCS. Capability gap, not a vendor complaint.)
5. IF A published business number, twenty or more employees, and none of the above -> Tier 5 - The Number Nobody Answers (The catch-all. No messaging program at all, so the entry point is the cheapest thing Signalmash sells: getting their company name to display on outbound calls.)

03 Tiers

Five campaigns, no overlap, nothing left uncovered

Tier 1 is the smallest and most valuable. Tier 5 is sixty percent of the market and takes the softest offer on the ladder. Every pain below is inferred directly from the trigger that routed the account, never invented, and every value proposition comes from something Signalmash actually ships.

Tier 1: The Platform Reselling Someone Else's Network

Product pages promise comms features to their own customers, such as two-way texting, appointment reminders or click-to-call, AND a competitor CPaaS is detected in the stack or on the published number.

Pain
They have made messaging a product feature but they do not own the network underneath it. Every sub-account needs its own brand and campaign registration. Every carrier filter becomes their support ticket rather than their vendor's. Margin is set by somebody else's rate card, and they find out about rate changes the same day their customers do. On top of that they carry compliance, billing and tax exposure for traffic they cannot see into.

06 Launch

Launch Tier 2 first, not Tier 1

Tier 2 is the largest confirmed-spend pool with the cleanest signal and the fastest route to a usable proof point. Tier 1 is worth more per deal but should wait until there is a result to put in the copy. Minimum fifteen hundred contacts before any tier is judged, because below that you cannot tell a bad tier from bad luck.

launch first
01

Weeks 1 to 3 - Launch Tier 2 at 1,500 contacts. Largest confirmed-spend pool, cleanest signal, fastest route to a usable proof point. Run the carrier-lookup open against the dated-review open as the first and most valuable test in the strategy.

02

Weeks 3 to 6 - Launch Tier 3 at 1,500 contacts. Small and time-sensitive, and the voice-AI window is open now. Re-scrape the directories monthly because this category is growing faster than any other segment on the list.

03

Weeks 5 to 9 - Launch Tier 1 at 1,500 contacts. Highest contract value on the ladder, but hold it until Tiers 2 and 3 have produced a proof point worth putting in the copy.

04

Weeks 8 to 12 - Launch Tier 4 at 2,000 contacts. Broader audience and a softer signal, and it needs the RCS asset finished before it goes out.

05

Week 12 onward - Launch Tier 5 at 2,500 contacts and up. Evergreen volume once the top of the ladder is stable.

06

Throughout - Separate sending domains for the technical buyer in Tiers 1 to 3 and the business buyer in Tiers 4 to 5, three to four mailboxes each, twenty-five to thirty sends per mailbox per day, warmed for three weeks before the first send.

07

Throughout - Run a full inbox-placement audit before launch and monthly after. This client sells communications deliverability, so if their own outbound lands in spam the pitch is dead on arrival.

08

Throughout - Route every technical reply to a real engineer within one business day. The entire pitch is that you get a human fast, so the outbound motion itself has to prove it.

09

At 120 days - Return non-responders to the pool, re-scraped. Both the carrier lookup and the consent-language field change over time, and a re-scrape can promote an account up the ladder, which makes the second touch stronger than the first rather than a repeat of it.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and push more volume into it.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run without changes.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test the second copy variant before touching the targeting.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500 sends Cut the tier and rework the trigger. Do not keep sending.
Fewer than 1,500 contacts in a tier Do not judge it at all. Below that volume you cannot tell a bad tier from bad luck.
