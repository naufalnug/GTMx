The signal

One signal: what just put a list on a clock

Not firmographics. The observable commercial event that forces a company to reach a population it does not hold — and every one of them is filed publicly.

strongest signal
01

It is an event, not an attribute. Attributes describe who a company is; events tell you what they have just committed to, which is what actually creates a purchase.

02

It is federally published and timestamped. An FDA clearance, a trial registration and a job posting all carry a date, so the outreach window is known rather than guessed.

03

It arrives with the payload attached. A 510(k) product code maps to a specialty panel, then to a provider taxonomy, then to an exact count of US providers by state — the list request writes itself.

04

It cannot be bought, so it cannot be copied. Competitors buy the same enrichment vendors as each other. Nobody is joining three federal datasets to prospect.

The routing

Six conditions, tested in strict order

Every account is tested top to bottom and routes to the first tier it matches. No account lands in two tiers, and the last tier catches everything else.

first match wins 
1. IF Suppress first: HQ in Germany or Austria, HQ in Canada without a documented consent basis, or an existing customer -> Excluded from the cascade entirely (German and Austrian competition law requires prior consent for B2B email with no legitimate-interest route, and CASL requires a documented basis per contact. Suppression happens before routing, not after.)
2. IF FDA clearance or approval in the trailing 180 days -> Tier 1 — Launch Clock (A clearance is the sharpest and most time-boxed event available. It wins over every other match because the email written from it is the hardest to ignore.)
3. IF Else, a Phase II or III trial recruiting, active, or read out inside the last year -> Tier 2 — Trial to Commercial (The commercial population after readout is far larger than the investigator base, and the file for it does not exist yet.)
4. IF Else, exhibiting at a relevant show inside the next 90 days -> Tier 3 — Booth Committed (Booth spend is already sunk against a fixed date, which creates a deadline the prospect feels without being told about it.)
5. IF Else, publicly named as a customer of a competing data vendor -> Tier 4 — Confirmed Payer (No need to argue that data is worth buying. The only question is why this data is different, which is a much easier email to write.)
6. IF Else, two or more open SDR, BDR or demand generation roles in 90 days -> Tier 5 — Headcount Ramp (New reps multiply list demand immediately, and per-seat or per-credit pricing punishes exactly this moment.)
7. IF Else — everything remaining -> Tier 6 — Execution Gap (catch-all) (The catch-all is what makes coverage complete. Without it the strategy leaves more than half the market unaddressed.)

The plays

What each tier actually runs

Every pain is inferred from its trigger, never invented. That traceability is what makes the copy read as observation rather than assumption.

volume + side quests
Niche Niche

Clearance to Taxonomy

The flagship play, and the one no competitor is running. It works because it requires joining three federal datasets a generic data vendor has no reason to touch — but B2B Lists already ingests NPPES, taxonomy codes and NPI validation for its healthcare product. The capability exists; it has simply never been pointed at prospecting.

Filter
Applicant companies with a 510(k), De Novo, PMA or drug approval decision dated inside the trailing 180 days
AI strategy
Chain three federal datasets into a single payload. The clearance record gives a three-letter product code; FDA Product Classification maps that to a medical specialty advisory panel; the panel maps to NUCC provider taxonomy codes; NPPES returns the exact count of US providers in that taxonomy, broken out by state with NPI and practice address. A clearance published on Tuesday becomes a fully specified list request by Wednesday.
Value
Arrive holding the list
Niche Niche

Pre-Readout Commercial File

The longest cycle in the cascade and the one to run last. Its strength is that provider data decays roughly two percent a month, so a file built under launch pressure is precisely the file that lets bad records through. Building early and maintaining is the pitch.

Filter
Industry-sponsored Phase II and III trials that are recruiting, active, or read out inside the last year
AI strategy
Pull lead sponsor, phase, status, condition and investigator site names from ClinicalTrials.gov, then size the full treating population for that indication from NPPES. The gap between the two numbers — investigator sites versus specialists treating the condition nationally — is the entire argument, and it is calculable before the first email.
Value
Be ready before the deadline exists
Focused Focused

Pre-Show, Post-Show

Strictly seasonal — this tier lives or dies by the show calendar, so it slots to show cycles rather than running continuously. The sunk booth cost does the persuading; the email only has to name the date.

Filter
Confirmed exhibitors at relevant healthcare and industrial shows with a show date inside 90 days
AI strategy
Scrape public exhibitor directories for company, booth and date, then build the target-title count among attending organisations. Two urgency angles get tested against each other: the countdown to the floor opening, and the well-known misery of the badge scan file arriving late and half-unusable.
Value
Book before the floor opens
Focused Focused

The Incumbent Audit

The strongest email in the set and the reason it launches first. It costs almost nothing to fulfil, it is very hard to refuse, and it puts B2B Lists in a conversation about a competitor's weaknesses using that competitor's own file as the evidence.

Filter
Companies publicly named as customers of a competing data vendor, via review authorship, vendor case studies or stack names in job requisitions
AI strategy
Skip the entire argument about whether data is worth buying — they already pay. Offer to run 500 rows of their current vendor's export through the eight-layer check and return exactly what is missing, stale or wrong. Free, no obligation, and they keep the report either way.
Value
Prove the gap with their own data
Broad Broad

Cost Per New Rep

The largest tier outside the catch-all, and it refreshes itself every 90 days as requisitions turn over. The real cost being named is not the licence — it is a new rep spending their first three weeks building lists instead of talking to people.

Filter
Companies with two or more open SDR, BDR or demand generation requisitions posted in the last 90 days
AI strategy
Do the arithmetic in the email. New reps multiplied by accounts each equals net-new records a month that somebody has to source, landing on a seat count sized for the old team. Then contrast per-seat and per-credit pricing against a flat-cost file, and offer 100 free records so a new hire has a working list on day one.
Value
Ramp reps in days, not weeks
Broad Broad

Free Teardown (A/B chaos test)

Over half the market by volume, with the lowest reply density per contact. The catch-all is not optional: without it the strategy would leave more accounts unaddressed than it covers.

Filter
Everything remaining in the market after the five tiers above
AI strategy
Two deliberately different variants run against each other, because there is no strong signal here to reason from. Variant A leads with the one technical read available — marketing sent from the primary domain with weak or absent DMARC. Variant B is soft and offers nothing but a free 500-row list health report.
Value
Give first, qualify later

The rollout

Nine weeks from build to full waterfall

Tier 4 launches first, not Tier 1 — it needs no new engineering and its offer is deliverable on day one, which buys time to build Tier 1 properly underneath it.

launch first
01

Weeks 0-2 — Build the infrastructure: 15 sending domains, 60 mailboxes, warmup running. Template the free-audit fulfilment so the offer can actually be serviced. Close the four launch blockers: restate the deliverability figure as a hard-bounce number with methodology, produce one outcome case study, build the public sample file, and evidence the compliance claims.

02

Week 3 — Launch Tier 4, Confirmed Payer. Deliberately not Tier 1: this tier needs no new engineering, its source is a straightforward scrape, and its offer is deliverable on day one. It buys the time to build Tier 1 properly underneath it.

03

Week 4 — Launch Tier 3, Booth Committed, but only if a relevant show falls inside 90 days. Otherwise it slots to the next show cycle rather than being forced.

04

Week 5 — Launch Tier 1, Launch Clock, once the FDA-to-taxonomy join is automated and fulfilment is templated. Build that join during weeks 3 and 4 while Tier 4 is already running.

05

Week 6 — Launch Tier 5, Headcount Ramp. The largest addressable volume outside the catch-all.

06

Week 8 — Launch Tier 6, the catch-all, running both variants against each other as a chaos test.

07

Week 9 — Launch Tier 2, Trial to Commercial. Longest sales cycle in the cascade, so it runs last and is judged over quarters.

08

Throughout — Hold the operating rules that a data company cannot break: a 1.0% hard bounce ceiling treated as a stop condition rather than a target, the verified bounce rate published in the email footer, no fallback merge fields ever, no sending from the primary domain, and no open tracking on Tiers 1 to 4 where enterprise security gateways flag pixels.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Excellent. Scale the tier and feed it more volume.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test exactly one copy variant, holding everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or under 1% reply after 1,500 sends Cut the tier and rework it. No sentiment.
Hard bounce above 1.0% on any send Stop the send. For a data company this is a stop condition, not a metric — above it, the pitch refutes itself.
Time from reply to file delivered above 24 hours Fix fulfilment before scaling. The 24-hour claim is what the whole campaign is built on.
