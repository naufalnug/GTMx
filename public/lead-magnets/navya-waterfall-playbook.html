Prepared for Navya Network

The Navya Network outbound journey.

One priority-routed waterfall across the US oncology decision market, built entirely on signals that CMS, the American College of Surgeons and the cancer programs themselves publish for free.

Core insight: Don't segment US oncology by title or facility type, because neither predicts intent. Route on how much an organization has already publicly committed to structured, multidisciplinary cancer decision-making, and where that commitment is visibly straining. That signal is a ladder, it is free to read, and the prospects publish it themselves.

01

The signal that predicts an oncology buyer

Commitment already placed, publicly and observably. A cancer program that runs nine tumor boards a week or signs a CMS risk contract has already told the market it takes the treatment decision seriously. That is the ladder we route on.

strongest signal
01

It is genuinely observable. CMS publishes its Enhancing Oncology Model participants by name, the American College of Surgeons publishes every accredited cancer program, and hospitals publish their own tumor board schedules to their own websites.

02

It ladders cleanly. Financial risk on the decision beats visible coordination load, which beats a tool already bought, which beats a headcount workaround, which beats a bare accreditation obligation.

03

It infers the pain honestly. Five boards a week means the calendar is the bottleneck. An open registrar req means someone senior is doing abstraction. We never invent a pain the trigger doesn't imply.

04

It costs nothing to maintain. The routing layer runs on public data, so the strategy can hold for twelve months while you just keep adding leads.

02

The lead-listing engine

Ten sources, none of them paid. The Commission on Cancer directory gives the market, CMS gives the highest-intent tier, and the hospitals' own tumor board calendars give the load signal. Contact discovery is the only real line item.

first match wins 
1. IF On the CMS Enhancing Oncology Model list, OR public evidence of an oncology value-based / bundled / risk contract, OR an ACO with an oncology service line -> Tier 1 - Risk on the Decision (Money is attached to guideline concordance. A hospital that also runs nine boards a week still routes here, because the financial signal is the one that gets a budget approved.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, AND a tumor board schedule is published listing 5 or more named disease-site boards per week -> Tier 2 - Tumor Board Load (Five boards a week is six to fifteen physician-hours of calendar-bound time. The bottleneck is visible on their own website, which makes it the most personalizable tier we have.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, AND a named oncology decision tool is detected (OncoLens, Navify Tumor Board, Ncompass, ClinicalPath / Via, Cota, Flatiron, Epic Beacon) -> Tier 3 - The Layer Above the Pathway (They already bought into structured decision-making. We are not a replacement, we are the synthesis and consensus layer their tool doesn't cover.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1 to 3, AND an open req exists for a Certified Tumor Registrar, Tumor Board Coordinator, Oncology Nurse Navigator or Oncology Data Specialist -> Tier 4 - Hiring the Manual Work (They are solving an abstraction problem with headcount, in a market where the credential pipeline is nationally short. The req is a dated, decaying signal, so refresh it weekly.)
5. IF Everything else in the TAM -> Tier 5 - Accredited Obligation (catch-all) (Accreditation obligates documented multidisciplinary case review whether or not any signal above is visible. The obligation exists regardless. No lead in the TAM is excluded.)

03

Five tiers, first match wins

Every account is tested top to bottom and lands in exactly one tier. The money signal outranks the load signal, because it is the one that gets a budget approved. The catch-all covers everything left.

On the CMS Enhancing Oncology Model participant list, or public evidence of an oncology value-based, bundled or risk contract, or an ACO with an oncology service line.

Pain

Source

Reply density
Highest intent in the waterfall. Budget is already attached. Smallest tier, so pair it with the payer side campaign to hold volume.
02 ~18% TAM

Tier 2: Tumor Board Load

A public tumor board or cancer conference schedule listing five or more named disease-site boards per week.

Pain
They run boards synchronously. Five boards a week is six to fifteen physician-hours of calendar-bound time. Most cases never get presented, and complex ones queue for the next slot. The board is the bottleneck, and it is published on their own website.
Offer
Tumor Board Load Audit: their own published schedule converted into annual physician-hours and cases-per-board capacity.
Source
Site scrape of tumor board / cancer conference / CME calendar pages, plus the CoC directory for the account list
Reply density
Strong. The best personalization in the entire waterfall, because you can name their actual boards back to them.
03 ~11% TAM

Tier 3: The Layer Above the Pathway

Not Tier 1 or 2, and a named oncology decision tool detected: OncoLens, Navify Tumor Board, Ncompass, ClinicalPath or Via, Cota, Flatiron, or an Epic Beacon pathway build.

Pain
They have already bought into digitized pathways or board scheduling. But nothing in that stack reads the raw record and produces a case summary, and nothing reconciles three specialists' opinions into one recommendation. The prep and the consensus are still manual, which is exactly why the tool underperforms its business case.
Offer
Structured Case Summary Sample: a live, de-identified case summary generated from a published complex case.
Source
Vendor customer and logo pages, press releases, job reqs naming the tool
Reply density
Good intent, longest cycle. They just bought something, so the conversation is about the layer above it, never a rip-and-replace.
04 ~13% TAM

Tier 4: Hiring the Manual Work

Not Tier 1 to 3, and an open requisition for a Certified Tumor Registrar, Tumor Board or Cancer Conference Coordinator, Oncology Nurse Navigator, or Oncology Data Specialist.

Pain
They are solving an abstraction-and-coordination problem with headcount, in a market where registrar reqs sit open for months because the credential pipeline is nationally short. Every week the req is open, someone considerably more expensive is doing the abstraction.
Offer
Time-to-Decision Benchmark: decision-stage delay mapped against the published evidence on treatment delay and outcomes.
Source
Hospital career sites, Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs keyword scrape
Reply density
Moderate and time-sensitive. The req closes and the window shuts, so this tier needs a weekly refresh or the trigger goes stale.
05 ~49% TAM

Tier 5: Accredited Obligation (catch-all)

Everything else in the TAM: every remaining accredited cancer program and community oncology practice.

Pain
Accreditation obligates documented multidisciplinary case review whether or not any signal above is publicly visible. The obligation exists; the infrastructure supporting it is whatever they have cobbled together.
Offer
The Cancer Treatment Decision Delay Report: the published case that the time before treatment starts is a clinical variable, not an administrative one.
Source
Commission on Cancer directory, Community Oncology Alliance directory
Reply density
Lowest per-send, highest absolute volume. This is where the durability of the strategy lives.

Launch order and thresholds

Start at Tier 2, not Tier 1. It has the best personalization in the whole waterfall and it validates the async-board message every other tier borrows from.

launch first
01

Weeks 1 to 3 - Launch Tier 2, Tumor Board Load. Start here, not at Tier 1. Tier 2 has the best personalization in the whole waterfall because you can name their actual boards, it has a healthy 4,700-contact pool, and it validates the asynchronous-board message every other tier borrows from.

02

Weeks 3 to 6 - Launch Tier 1 alongside the payer side campaign. Tier 1 is small at ~2,600 contacts, and the payer campaign carries the same risk-and-money argument at volume, where the ASCO 2020 data is strongest.

03

Weeks 6 to 9 - Turn on Tier 5, the catch-all. This is the volume engine. Switch it on once the two sharp tiers have told you which framing travels.

04

Weeks 9 to 12 - Launch Tiers 3 and 4. Tier 3 needs the sample case-summary asset built first. Tier 4 needs a weekly job-requisition refresh running, or the trigger goes stale.

05

Week 12 and beyond - Layer on the remaining side campaigns: expert supply, conference-triggered windows, and the new-service-line-leader monthly refresh.

06

Infrastructure baseline: four to six sending domains, three mailboxes each, 14-day warmup, 20 to 25 sends per mailbox per day. That yields roughly 8,000 to 10,000 sends per month.

07

Suppression: every existing customer, every expert panel member, every Tata Memorial and National Cancer Grid affiliate, and any .gov address unless legal has cleared it.

08

Compliance guardrails, non-negotiable: never imply the platform makes the treatment decision, because it produces options and consensus for a physician; never reference an identifiable patient or case in any merge field; and every metric in copy must trace to a published source.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Perfect. Scale the tier and add contacts before touching the copy.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test the second copy variant. Do not rebuild the tier.
Worse than 1 per 500, or under 1% reply rate after 1,500 sends Failing. Cut and rework, and check the trigger before you blame the copy.
Reply latency Clinical and service-line buyers reply in 4 to 14 days. Do not judge a tier before day 21.
Negative sentiment above 0.5% of replies Pause that tier regardless of its reply rate. Clinical buyers complain loudly and to colleagues.
