The signal

The agency name is in the footer

Dental and aesthetic web vendors put their credit line in the contract, so every practice site names its incumbent agency in plain text. That is a competitor client list for the whole industry, free, refreshable, and unbuyable.

strongest signal
01

It is published, not inferred. The vendor credit is a contract term, so it sits in plain text on the practice's own homepage.

02

No data broker sells it. BuiltWith indexes that a site runs WordPress, not that PBHS built it and still bills for it monthly.

03

It refreshes itself. Practices change vendors constantly, and every switch reshuffles accounts between tiers on the next crawl.

04

It pairs with two other free public sources. Google's Ads Transparency Center proves the budget; a live rank check proves whether it is working.

Direct page-source crawl (the unlock)

Vendor footer credit, surgeon roster, location count, referral-page presence, video, press and blog presence. Crawls /, /about, /meet-the-doctors, /referrals and /contact. This one cannot be substituted.

02

AAOMS Surgeon Locator (myoms.org/find-a-surgeon)

The oral surgery core market: surgeon, practice, city, contact. Public with no login, but rendered client-side, so it needs a headless browser rather than a plain fetch.

03

AAP, ACP, ABPS/ASPS and AAFPRS directories

The adjacent specialty market: periodontics, prosthodontics, plastic and facial plastic surgery, all sitting on the same signal ladder.

04

Google Ads Transparency Center + Meta Ad Library

Confirmed live paid spend, ad creative, launch dates and targeted regions. Both free, both searchable by practice name, neither requires a login.

05

Live rank check, 8 to 12 money terms per metro

Who actually holds the top three on the terms that drive consults. This is both the second half of every tier's story and the entire lead magnet.

06

Google Maps / Places by specialty and metro

Practice entity, location count, review count and velocity, website URL. Supplies the patient-volume proxy used by the qualification gate.

07

Becker's Dental Review / Group Dentistry Now

Dated acquisition and de-novo location events, free, feeding the side campaigns rather than the cascade.

The routing

Ordered by the size of the bet already placed

Vendor commitment, then demonstrated ad spend, then visibility outcome. Each practice is tested top to bottom and exits at the first tier it matches, so nobody gets two pitches.

first match wins 
1. IF Qualification gate, applied before any routing: real practice domain, at least one board-certified specialist, 50+ Google reviews at the primary location OR two or more locations, US-based -> Enter the cascade (Nuvolum engagements start around $3,000/month. A solo practice collecting $700k cannot clear that, and mailing them burns sending reputation for nothing. Roughly 30 to 35% of the raw directory pull drops here, which is the gate working rather than a loss.)
2. IF location_count >= 3 OR group_affiliated == TRUE, AND vendor_class == volume_template -> TIER 1 - Multi-site practice, single-site website (A multi-site surgical group running on a website product built for one practice. Locations compete with their own siblings for the same metro terms, so authority splits instead of compounding. The incumbent vendor cannot fix this because the vendor does not sell that.)
3. IF paid_ads_live == TRUE AND money_term_rank NOT IN top 3 -> TIER 2 - Renting the term you should own (Two verified public facts, and the pain is arithmetic on them. They are paying per click for a term they should own outright, so paid spend has become the floor rather than the ceiling.)
4. IF vendor_class == premium_custom AND (has_video == FALSE OR has_press_page == FALSE OR blog_last_post_date older than 180 days) -> TIER 3 - You bought a website, not a growth engine (The custom vendor credit proves budget was spent. A missing /video, /press or stale /blog route is a reliable absence signal because the route either resolves or it does not, unlike inferring absence from a tech-stack index.)
5. IF specialty IN (oms, perio, prostho) AND has_referral_page == FALSE -> TIER 4 - Your biggest case channel is unmanaged (That oral surgery case volume flows through referring general dentists is the structural fact of the specialty. The referral page either exists or it does not, and without one that channel has no digital front door at all.)
6. IF Everything else in the addressable market -> TIER 5 - CATCH-ALL - Three practices own your metro (A practice already ranking top three with video, press and a live blog falls all the way here by design. They do not have a visibility problem, and manufacturing one would be the exact invented-pain failure this framework exists to prevent.)

Five campaigns, five honest pains

Every pain point traces straight back to its trigger. Nothing is inferred about the market and nothing is invented about the practice.

Tier 1: Multi-site practice, single-site website

Three or more locations, or DSO/MSO affiliation, AND on a volume or template web vendor (PBHS, ProSites, Officite, Dentalfone, Great Dental Websites)

Pain
They run a multi-site surgical group on a website product built for a single practice. Each location competes with its own siblings for the same metro terms, authority splits instead of compounding, and no location page is strong enough to win its own market. The incumbent vendor cannot fix this, because the vendor does not sell that.

Source

Reply density
Highest contract value in the program. Smaller list, and the tier where the Beacon reference does the most work.

Tier 2: Renting the term you should own

Live ads confirmed in Google Ads Transparency Center or the Meta Ad Library, AND outside the top three for their primary money term

Pain
They are paying per click for a term they should own outright. Paid spend has become the floor rather than the ceiling, so cost per consult only rises. And the moment the card stops, the pipeline stops with it.

Source
Google Ads Transparency Center and Meta Ad Library, plus a live rank check on the metro's money terms
Reply density

03 11% TAM

Tier 3: You bought a website, not a growth engine

On a premium or custom vendor (Rosemont Media, Progressive Dental, O360, Studio III), AND missing video, a press page, or a blog updated in the last 180 days

Pain
They invested real money in a brand and then had nothing to feed it. The site is a brochure that was finished, not a channel that runs. The three things that actually convert a high-ticket elective consult, patient story video, earned media and consistent content, are absent or stalled.
Offer
Content Gap Audit - what exists on the three channels that convert elective consults, scored against the three highest-visibility practices in their metro
Source
Page-source crawl for vendor class, video embeds, /press and /media routes, and the most recent blog post date
Reply density
Sells Nuvolum's highest-margin services. Needs benchmark scoring built per metro first.
04 9% TAM

Tier 4: Your biggest case channel is unmanaged

Oral surgery, periodontics or prosthodontics, AND no referring-doctor page or portal anywhere on the site

Pain
Their highest-value case volume, implants, third molars and full-arch, arrives via referring general dentists, and that channel has no digital front door at all. Referrals come by fax and phone, nobody can tell which referrers have gone quiet, and a competitor with a portal is the lower-friction choice for the referring office's front desk.
Offer
Referral Catchment Map - every general dental practice within a ten-minute drive of each of their offices, and which of those already name a specialist referral relationship on their own site
Source
Page-source crawl for /refer, /referrals, /referring-doctors and /professionals routes, plus a Google Places radius query per office
Reply density
The most differentiated tier in the program and the one no competitor is running. Also the heaviest magnet to produce.
05 58% TAM

Tier 5: Catch-all: three practices own your metro

Everything else in the addressable market

Pain
Their metro's elective search demand is concentrated in two or three practices and they are not one of them. Nothing is visibly broken, which is exactly the problem. There is no event forcing a decision, and the gap compounds quietly while the practice assumes referrals and reputation will hold.
Offer
Metro Visibility Report - every qualifying practice in their metro ranked across the money terms, with their own position marked
Source
Live rank check across the metro, plus the specialty directory pull for the full local practice set
Reply density
58% of the market and the softest ask in the program. This is the tier still producing in month eleven.

The rollout

Tier 2 first, not Tier 1

Tier 1 carries the highest contract value but takes a week of group mapping. Tier 2 needs two instant public lookups, so it produces the first reply while the rest is still being built.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-2 - Launch Tier 2 first, not Tier 1. It needs only two instant public lookups, it carries the largest sharp-tier volume in the market, its first line is the most verifiable in the program, and it directly proves the metric Nuvolum leads with. Fastest route to a reply worth showing the client.

02

Weeks 3-4 - Tier 1. Highest contract value in the program and the tier where the Beacon Oral Specialists reference does the most work. Launch as soon as the multi-location and DSO-parentage mapping is clean, which is roughly a week of reconciling the crawl against deal records.

03

Weeks 5-6 - Tier 5, the catch-all. Turn this on early rather than last. It is 58% of the market and the softest ask in the program, so it should be accumulating replies in the background while the sharp tiers are still being tuned.

04

Weeks 7-8 - Tier 3. Needs the content-gap scoring built against three benchmark practices per metro. Worth the wait, because it sells Nuvolum's highest-margin services.

05

Weeks 9-10 - Tier 4. Needs the general-dentist catchment build, a Places radius query per office. The most differentiated tier in the program and the one no competitor is running, but the heaviest magnet to produce.

06

Month 2 onwards - Side campaigns. New-associate detection goes live once a month of crawl snapshots exists, so start snapshotting on day one.

07

Month-one deliverable: the Tier 2 list plus the first fifty Metro Money-Term Maps. Those maps are simultaneously the routing evidence, the lead magnet, and the proof that the data layer works.

08

Before scaling, close three proof gaps. The 91% top-three claim needs a denominator (how many keywords, which practice, what period). Tier 4's case-acceptance figures need attribution to a named practice with a date. And Tiers 1 and 2 are both displacement pitches with no published displacement story. One conversation with Nuvolum's data team naming a single client, the incumbent vendor replaced, the keyword set and the before-and-after unblocks 22% of the market and the two highest-value tiers.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale this tier hard and pour list volume into it.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test the second copy variant before touching anything else.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Cut the tier and rework the trigger or the magnet. Do not rewrite the copy first - a dead tier is almost always a trigger problem, not a wording problem.
Testing discipline Two copy variants per tier, maximum. Tiers 2 and 5 launch with no prior signal, so run a chaos test of two genuinely different approaches, then narrow to single-variable tests once a winner emerges.
