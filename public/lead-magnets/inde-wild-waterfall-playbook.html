The signal

What they already sell, or already do with their hands

A retailer's brand index is purchase-history intent sitting in public HTML. No data vendor sells it, and it costs nothing to read.

strongest signal

The routing

One cascade, first match wins

Every account is tested top-down and routes to exactly one campaign. The final tier catches everything else, so no account in the market is left unaddressed.

first match wins 
1. IF door_count ≥ 10 or account is a distributor, AND the brand index already lists an adjacent Ayurvedic or South-Asian-founded brand -> Tier 1 — Proven Category, Missing Brand (They bet on the category and it turned. The shelf has a proven segment with no #1 SKU in it, and their competitor set just got the Sephora-validated one.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1, AND the brand index lists an adjacent brand -> Tier 2 — Already Sells the Category (They've already paid to educate their customer on Ayurvedic haircare and are sending the sell-through to a brand with no clinical file behind it.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2, AND independent retailer / grocery-beauty / wellness clinic, AND no adjacent brand stocked, AND catchment in the top 3 deciles for South Asian population -> Tier 3 — Demand Walks Past the Door (The demand is in their postcode and it's being ordered on Amazon and Nykaa instead of walking into the store.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1-3, AND the published service menu names an oil, scalp, champi, abhyanga, shirodhara or bridal-hair service, AND a retail shelf is present -> Tier 4 — You Do the Ritual, You Don't Sell It (They perform the treatment, the client asks what was used, and the answer generates zero revenue. Service margin is capped by chair hours. Retail margin isn't.)
5. IF NOT Tier 1-4, AND the account is a professional or bridal artist -> Tier 5 — The Kit Buyer (Buying the kit at full retail, one bride at a time, and posting the results for free.)
6. IF Everything else in the beauty and wellness retail universe -> Tier 6 — Catch-All: Tester Kit (No signal. Weakest intent, broadest copy, softest ask — a free tester kit with no order attached.)

The rollout

Learn on the cheap tier, spend on the expensive one

Tier 4 launches first — 21,000 accounts and no dependency on a wholesale program existing yet. Tier 1 is the most valuable and runs manually, capped at 40 accounts a month.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-3 — Launch Tier 4 (You Do the Ritual). Counter-intuitive but correct: 21,000 accounts, a clean scrapeable signal, and the shortest distance between trigger and pain. It's also the only major tier that doesn't depend on a wholesale program existing yet.

02

Weeks 2-5 — Run Tier 1 in parallel, hybrid and manual, capped at 40 accounts a month. Founder-signed email, hand-built Shelf Report, LinkedIn touch on the buyer. Highest revenue per win, so it should never absorb unproven copy.

03

Weeks 4-7 — Launch Tier 2, then Tier 3. Tier 2 first because it's smaller and warmer: prove the retail copy on 2,400 accounts before spending Tier 3's 9,800 on it.

04

Weeks 7-10 — Launch Tier 5 (The Kit Buyer). Highest volume, lowest value per win. Blocked until the pro artist program actually exists.

05

Week 10 onward — Launch Tier 6, the catch-all. Only spend the remaining 45,000 accounts once the copy has been proven upstream.

06

Before anything ships: build a one-page wholesale sheet and a one-page pro artist program. These are operational gaps, not copy gaps, and they gate roughly 60% of the addressable market.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Excellent. Scale the tier and add leads aggressively.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run unchanged.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test a copy variant before adding volume.
Worse than 1 per 500, or under 1% reply rate after 1,500 sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the angle.
Tier 1 (any rate) Exempt. At ~350 accounts worldwide it will never produce meaningful reply data. Measure on buyer meetings booked instead.
Tier 5 (any rate) Exempt. High reply rate, low revenue per reply. Measure on activated artists × kit reorder rate.
