01 / Signal

Research organisations publish their own procurement stack

The two most expensive fields to enrich in a normal B2B list, what system do they run and how fragmented is their spend, are both free here. Enrichment budget goes entirely to contact data.

strongest signal
01

Public universities and academic medical centres are obliged to publish supplier information. Their procurement pages name the branded marketplace, BearBuy, UCIBuy, Shell Shop, eShop, which maps one-to-one to the underlying P2P platform.

02

Those same pages list the full set of preferred lab suppliers with punchout catalogs, which means the fragmentation count is a number you can literally read off the page.

03

Commercial biopharma publishes the same thing to a different audience, through supplier-onboarding and vendor-registration pages, because vendors must register inside the procurement platform.

04

The signal is simultaneously the intent signal, the qualification signal and the objection-handling signal. An account listing six punchouts has already won the internal argument for centralised lab buying and already failed to solve comparison.

02 / Routing

One cascade, first match wins, nothing falls through

Every account exits the matrix with exactly one tier. If any account exits with no tier, the catch-all is broken and nothing sends until it is fixed.

first match wins 
1. IF punchout_supplier_count is 4 or more AND p2p_platform is known -> Tier 1, Fragmentation Proven (They bought the procurement layer, then bolted four to eight separate supplier storefronts onto it. The integration objection is already answered and the comparison problem is already proven unsolved.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1 AND (a published diverse-spend target exists OR federal_contractor_flag is true) -> Tier 2, Mandate On The Clock (They have committed publicly to a number they must report against, but the vendor decision is made by a scientist who cannot see which supplier is certified.)
3. IF NOT Tiers 1-2 AND a procurement or lab-ops requisition was posted in the last 60 days -> Tier 3, Solving It With Headcount (They have concluded the fix is another person doing order entry. That role gets consumed by transactional work and never reaches strategic sourcing.)
4. IF NOT Tiers 1-3 AND (Series B+ within 12 months OR a facility expansion event OR NIH award growth above 15% YoY) -> Tier 4, Spend About To Scale (Unit economics lock in during the first year of a ramp. Setting them up front is a configuration; renegotiating after eighteen months of habits is a project.)
5. IF NOT Tiers 1-4 AND (any NIH funding OR R&D headcount of 10 or more) -> Tier 5, Catch-All: The Benchmark (No observable trigger exists, so any claim of account-specific insight would be manufactured. We trade on category data instead and let the magnet do the qualifying.)
6. IF No wet lab, no NIH funding, R&D headcount under 10 -> Suppress (Outside the addressable market. There is no reagent spend to unify.)

06 / Launch

Deliberately inverted, to protect the finite tiers

There are roughly 2,400 Tier 1 accounts in the world and they do not renew. They get validated copy, never test copy. Tier 5 is where we calibrate.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-2, Build. Stand up the procurement-page crawler, join it to NIH RePORTER and BRIMR, and run contact enrichment. This produces the routing matrix. Nothing sends in this phase.

02

Weeks 3-5, Calibrate on Tier 5. Launch the catch-all at 4,000 contacts as a chaos test of two entirely different copies. It is the largest and most replaceable tier, it establishes the deliverability baseline on .edu and .gov, and it produces the reply data that de-risks Tier 1.

03

Weeks 5-8, Rolling triggers. Launch Tiers 3 and 4. Both refresh continuously, so mistakes are recoverable. This is where we confirm that trigger-based personalisation actually lifts reply rate above the Tier 5 baseline.

04

Weeks 8-11, The prize. Launch Tier 1, then Tier 2, and only once the personalisation lift is confirmed and the sending infrastructure is warm and clean. Start Tier 1 at 800 contacts, not all 8,400.

05

Week 12 onward, Steady state. All five tiers running with a weekly refresh. Tiers 1 and 2 top up as new procurement pages publish, Tiers 3 and 4 refresh weekly, Tier 5 absorbs the remainder. The ongoing work is a crawler re-run and a contact top-up, not a campaign rebuild.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and add more contacts per account.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched and resist the urge to optimise.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test exactly one copy variant and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the trigger, not the copy.
Tier 5 at 1 per 400-600 Acceptable, and do not cut it. One UC-scale account justifies the entire year's send volume. Judge this tier on pipeline value, not reply ratio.
Any .edu or .gov sending Open tracking off, no links in email 1, slow warm ramp. Deliverability is the binding constraint here, not copy quality.
