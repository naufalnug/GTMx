Signal

The routing signal nobody sells

Apollo, ZoomInfo and Clay's stock providers have no filter for “hosts a 34MB catalogue PDF last touched sixteen months ago.” This list is derived, not bought — a competitor can copy the idea but cannot buy the output.

strongest signal
01

It proves the use case, not just the firmographic. A 300-person manufacturer with no catalogue is not a prospect. A 60-person wholesaler with a 40-page line sheet is.

02

It is self-refreshing. PDFs get replaced and publications get new issues — and a new asset is a hotter trigger than a stale one, because it proves someone is being paid to run the workflow.

03

It hands you the personalisation for free. You can name the file, its byte size and its last-modified date. Nothing in the email can be wrong, because it came off their own server.

04

It is unbuyable and therefore durable. Competitors can copy the idea but not the output. The moat is the crawler, not the copy.

The prospect's own domain (site-scoped PDF search + HTTP HEAD)

Every public PDF, its exact byte size, its last-modified date and its page count — which is the Tier 3 trigger, the personalisation and the lead magnet, all from one request.

02

Publicly indexed publications with the publisher's name, title, page count and publish date. Selection bias works in your favour: the discoverable accounts are the free and lower tiers — the ones running third-party ads on their own brand.

03

FlippingBook, Publitas, Yumpu, Calaméo and Paperturn public viewers

Confirmed payers on a named competitor, with the specific publication to reference and a publish date that tells you where they sit in the renewal cycle.

04

DOL Form 5500 filings

Every US employer with 200+ plan participants, with plan size and broker attached. This is the entire Tier 4 open-enrollment list, free, from a federal file.

05

Trade association member lists, exhibitor directories, IPEDS

The seed universe by category, without touching a data vendor. Contact discovery happens last and only for routed accounts — you pay for contacts on ~30% of the list instead of 100%.

06

Flipsnack's own product database

Which corporate domains already have two or more separate low-tier accounts on them. Zero cost, zero enrichment, and structurally invisible to every competitor.

Waterfall

How every account gets routed

First match wins, evaluated top down, and a qualification gate runs before anything routes. Every qualified account lands in exactly one tier. Nothing is orphaned and nothing is double-counted.

first match wins 
1. IF Under 50 employees, an agency or freelancer, an existing Team/Enterprise customer, or based in Germany -> Drop, divert to CS, or route to LinkedIn and paid only (Below Team-plan economics, or a reseller rather than a buyer, or already a customer. Germany is a hard exclusion: UWG §7 requires prior consent for B2B commercial email and Flipsnack is an EU sender, so there is no third-country ambiguity to shelter behind.)
2. IF Two or more distinct users on the same corporate domain hold separate Free/Starter/Professional Flipsnack accounts -> Tier 1 — Product-Qualified Expansion (The highest-intent segment in the entire market. The product is already in the building and has already won on merit. You are not selling flipbooks, you are selling consolidation.)
3. IF A live publication for the account is found on Issuu, FlippingBook, Publitas, Yumpu, Calaméo or Paperturn -> Tier 2 — Competitor-Hosted Publication (Confirmed budget, confirmed use case, confirmed publishing cadence — and you can name the exact publication and its publish date in the first line.)
4. IF A catalogue, brochure, lookbook, line sheet, benefits guide, annual report, price list, viewbook or prospectus PDF sits on their own domain at over 8MB or over 20 pages -> Tier 3 — The Stale Heavy PDF (The largest tier, and the only one where the personalisation is free, specific and impossible to fake. Scored up if the file was last modified more than nine months ago.)
5. IF A Tier 2 or Tier 3 account is currently inside a documented publication window (open enrollment, market week, annual report season, viewbook season) -> Tier 4 — The Publishing Calendar (The asset is the same; the clock supplies the reason to reply this week. The account is pulled out of its base tier for the length of the window and returns when it closes — never in two tiers at once.)
6. IF Passes the gate but matches nothing above -> Tier 5 — Catch-all (Routed on firmographics only, and the copy must not claim personalisation it does not have. This tier is the R&D budget: replies here become new tier definitions.)

Tiers

Five tiers, hottest to coldest

The order is intent, not volume. Tier 1 is the smallest list in the plan and the highest-converting; Tier 3 is half the market and funds everything else.

click each card
01 2-4% TAM

Tier 1: Product-Qualified Expansion

Two or more distinct users on the same corporate email domain holding separate Free, Starter or Professional Flipsnack accounts — or a single account whose flipbooks are embedded on a corporate domain at meaningful view volume.

Pain
Three departments each paying $456 a year on separate cards, four different brand treatments in market, no SSO, no shared asset library and no cross-team analytics — and procurement does not know any of it exists. When someone leaves, their flipbooks leave with them, because the files sit in a personal account.
Offer
Seat Consolidation Map — every account on their domain, total current spend, against the Team price
Source
Flipsnack's own product database. Zero enrichment, zero cost, structurally invisible to competitors. Blocked on an internal data pull, which is why it launches last.
Reply density
Highest in the cascade. Smallest list in the plan.
02 12-18% TAM

Tier 2: Competitor-Hosted Publication

A live publication for the account found on issuu.com, online.flippingbook.com, view.publitas.com, yumpu.com, calameo.com or paperturn-view.com.

Pain
On Issuu's free plan the reader serves third-party ads and the publication is public by default — so there is a reasonable chance a competitor's ad is running next to page 12 of their own catalogue. On the lower tiers they get view counts and almost nothing else: not which pages people stopped on, not which products got clicked, not who came back.
Offer
Side-by-Side Rebuild — the first six pages of one of their live publications, rebuilt and sent as a link, not a deck
Source
The competitors' own public directories. Issuu publisher profiles and category browse are indexed; FlippingBook, Publitas and Paperturn serve viewers on public subdomains.
Reply density
High. Confirmed payers with a named, dated publication to reference.
03 45-55% TAM

Tier 3: The Stale Heavy PDF

A catalogue, brochure, lookbook, line sheet, benefits guide, annual report, price list, viewbook or prospectus PDF on their own domain — linked from primary nav or a resources page — at over 8MB or over 20 pages. Scored up past nine months since last modified.

Pain
A 34MB PDF on a 4G phone is roughly a 40-second download before anything appears, and most people leave. You cannot see who opened it, which page they stopped on, or which product they kept returning to. And when a price changes you re-export the whole file, at which point every link anyone has ever shared points at the wrong version.
Offer
PDF Weight Report — every public PDF on their domain, with size, age, estimated mobile load time and the three worst offenders
Source
A site-scoped PDF search plus one HTTP HEAD request per file. No enrichment vendor is touched at any point, and the report is generated by the same crawler that built the list.
Reply density
Medium-high, at very high volume. The volume engine of the plan.
04 15-20% TAM

Tier 4: The Publishing Calendar

A Tier 2 or Tier 3 account currently inside a documented window: US open enrollment (Aug-Oct), retail market weeks and seasonal line sheets (Jan and Jul), annual and impact reports (Feb-Apr for December year ends, Sep-Nov for June), higher-ed viewbooks and course catalogues (Feb-Apr).

Pain
The guide, catalogue or report is being rebuilt right now, on a deadline, by someone who already knows the PDF version does not work on a phone and cannot tell them which sections got read. Every October the same questions land in the HR inbox because nobody knows what was actually opened.
Offer
Window-specific — an Open Enrollment Guide Rebuild built from their own prior-year public guide, or a Line Sheet to Shoppable Catalogue demo
Source
DOL Form 5500 filings for the open-enrollment list, fiscal year ends for report season, academic calendars for higher ed. The account is pulled out of its base tier for the window and returns when it closes.
Reply density
High in-window, zero out of window. The clock is the reason to reply.
05 remainder TAM

Tier 5: Catch-all

Passes the qualification gate but matched nothing above. Routed on firmographics only: retail, wholesale, manufacturing, education, hospitality, non-profit or financial services, 50 to 5,000 employees, with a marketing or communications function.

Pain
Unknown by definition — which is exactly why the copy must not claim personalisation it does not have. No “I saw your...”. Lead with the category observation and the artefact. Faked personalisation in a catch-all tier is what burns a sending domain.
Offer
Digital Publishing Benchmark for their category
Source
Trade association member lists, exhibitor directories and IPEDS. No signal layer by definition.
Reply density
Low, but the highest learning value. Replies here become new tier definitions.

Launch

Launch order and thresholds

Tier 2 first because it is the sharpest message and the smallest build. Tier 1 launches last despite being the most valuable, because it is blocked on an internal data pull.

launch first
01

Tier 2 first. Confirmed payers, the sharpest message, and the smallest build — a competitor directory scrape only. It validates the entire positioning inside two weeks and produces the displacement case study the rest of the campaign is currently missing. Launch on the ad-safety angle, which needs no case study, while the proof is being built.

02

Tier 3 second, once the PDF crawler is running. This is the volume engine and it funds everything else. The crawler that builds the list also builds the artefact, so marginal cost per prospect is effectively zero.

03

Tier 4 switched on when the calendar opens. Start with US open enrollment, because the Form 5500 list is free and the two existing case studies sit in exactly that use case. Gated on written BAA confirmation.

04

Tier 1 last, gated on the internal data pull from Flipsnack RevOps. It is the highest-value tier in the plan, but it is blocked on client-side engineering — do not let it hold up launch.

05

Tier 5 continuous from week one at low volume, as the control group and the R&D channel. Its job is to find the next tier definition, not to close deals.

06

Infrastructure: three sending domains, Flipsnack-adjacent but never the primary. flipsnack.com carries transactional and product-notification mail for a large free base — a spam complaint on cold outbound must never be able to reach password-reset deliverability. Three mailboxes per domain, 20 sends each per day after a three-week ramp of 5, 10, 15, 20.

07

Open tracking off on all EU sends. A tracking pixel is personal-data processing and Flipsnack is an EU controller — do not create the exposure for a vanity metric. Measure on reply rate and positive reply rate, never opens.

08

Before any tier sends: close the proof gaps. Fourteen named case studies currently carry zero quantified outcomes, and no customer is publicly named as having switched from Issuu or FlippingBook. One working session closes most of it.

Scale / cut rules

Under 2% reply after 400 sends in a tier Rewrite email one. Keep the targeting — the signal is not the problem yet.
Under 1% reply after 800 sends The signal is wrong, not the copy. Re-scope the tier definition.
Over 6% positive reply Scale sending volume 2x. Do not touch the copy.
Bounce over 3% Stop the tier immediately — the list build is picking up role accounts.
Spam complaints over 0.08% Pause the sending domain and rotate.
Meeting to opportunity under 25% The tier is attracting the wrong persona. Fix the persona map, not the email.
