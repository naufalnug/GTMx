02

How the routing works

Each account is tested top to bottom and routes to the first tier it matches. Exclusive, priority-ordered, and 100% covered by a catch-all — so no qualified enterprise account sits outside the strategy.

first match wins 
1. IF Ranks top 5 on Google for a commercial head term AND is cited in zero of ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity AND a named competitor is cited in at least two -> Tier 1 — Rank Rich, AI Invisible (The sharpest provable finding available. They won the old game and a competitor is already occupying their co-citation cluster.)
2. IF NOT Tier 1 AND picked up 25 or more new referring domains in 90 days AND DR-to-traffic ratio is top quartile OR topical coherence is below threshold -> Tier 2 — Authority Bought, Not Flowing (Budget is confirmed and already moving. The argument is that they are being sold Domain Rating rather than authority flow.)
3. IF NOT Tier 1 or 2 AND ranks between 4 and 30 for a head term with 10,000+ monthly search volume -> Tier 3 — Stalled at the Gate (Knocking on the door of a term worth owning. The Stake.com rank 30 to #1 story maps onto their exact situation.)
4. IF NOT Tier 1, 2 or 3 AND has a Head of SEO or Organic in seat OR an open SEO / GEO / AI-search role OR has published on AI search -> Tier 4 — Mandate Without Infrastructure (The mandate and budget exist. The missing half is data science, which cannot be hired for on a normal timeline.)
5. IF Everything else that passes the enterprise gate -> Tier 5 — Catch-All: Enterprise Organic Footprint (Carries 47% of the market. Without it roughly 11,300 qualified accounts would sit outside the strategy entirely.)

Launch and measurement

Tier 1 launches first because it answers the single most important unknown: whether the AI-citation argument sells to a buyer who is currently winning on Google.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-3 — Build. Stand up 25-30 secondary domains with 3 mailboxes each, all forwarding to wldm.io, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured before warmup starts. Run the SERP pull across ~500 head terms in 12 verticals, apply the enterprise gate, and execute the AI citation sweep on Tier 1 candidates. Gate to advance: 24,000 accounts routed and 1,700 Tier 1 accounts carrying citation data.

02

Weeks 4-5 — Launch Tier 1 with the chaos test across ~4,250 contacts. Two fundamentally different Day-0 emails, split evenly. Gate to advance: 1,500+ sends delivered and a readable reply ratio.

03

Weeks 6-7 — Launch Tier 2 and narrow Tier 1 to the winning copy. Gate to advance: Tier 1 running at 1 positive reply per 450 contacts or better.

04

Weeks 8-9 — Launch Tier 3 and build the core-update fallout side campaign so it is ready to fire the day an update is confirmed. Gate to advance: lead magnet fulfilment holding inside 72 hours.

05

Weeks 10-11 — Launch Tier 4.

06

Week 12 onward — Launch the catch-all at full volume, reaching 2,500 sends per day across 80-90 mailboxes. Full market runway is roughly 24 weeks across ~60,000 contacts.

07

Ongoing — Release the remaining side campaigns only once the waterfall is stable. Fulfilment capacity, not send volume, is the real constraint on this strategy: a missed 72-hour promise costs more credibility than a slower ramp costs pipeline.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale the tier and add lead volume.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Deploy the second copy variant, changing one variable only.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate below 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework the trigger or the offer.
Enterprise adjustment These benchmarks come from high-volume outbound against self-serve offers. WLDM sells project-based engagements with a three-month minimum, so a single close justifies a materially worse ratio. Do not cut a tier on ratio alone if it is producing qualified enterprise conversations — read pipeline value alongside reply ratio.
