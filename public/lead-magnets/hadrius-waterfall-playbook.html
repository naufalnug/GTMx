The signal

The regulator publishes the buyer list

Form ADV Item 1.J gives us the CCO's name, phone and email — self-reported by the firm and legally required to stay current. The same filing carries AUM, headcount, branch count, disciplinary history, whether the CCO is dual-hatted, and every social handle the firm has registered.

The routing

One question, six tiers, 100% coverage

How much regulatory pressure is this firm visibly under right now, and what is it visibly using to absorb it? Every account is tested top-to-bottom and routes to the first tier it matches, so each firm gets exactly one campaign.

first match wins 
1. IF A SEC, FINRA or state disciplinary action, deficiency letter or new ADV Item 11 disclosure lands in the last 12 months -> Tier 1 — Regulatory Event Live (The strongest signal in the market. Remediation is funded, on the calendar, and someone senior is watching. Urgency outranks every other trigger, so this fires first even if the firm also names an incumbent.)
2. IF Otherwise, a job post, ADV brochure or website names ComplySci, MCO, Smarsh, Global Relay, COMPLY, Orion Compliance, ACA, SEI or an archiving vendor -> Tier 2 — Named Incumbent Stack (The named tool proves budget exists and the category is understood. We only detect an incumbent where it is positively stated — never by inferring absence, which is not reliably detectable for archiving.)
3. IF Otherwise, the CCO is dual-hatted on Schedule A, or compliance staff are under 3% of headcount, or there is an open compliance req at a sub-250-person firm -> Tier 3 — Under-Resourced Compliance Function (One person is carrying an entire supervision program part-time. The largest high-quality tier, and the one where copy validates the load rather than selling software.)
4. IF Otherwise, the site carries testimonials, third-party ratings, performance figures or award badges (or publishes 2+ pieces a month) AND the ADV lists 2+ social handles -> Tier 4 — Marketing Rule Exposure (High advertising volume under Rule 206(4)-1 with review almost certainly running through email and a spreadsheet. Testimonials on an RIA site are themselves a post-2021 Marketing Rule artifact.)
5. IF Otherwise, the firm registered with the SEC in the last 12 months, added two or more branch offices, or went through M&A or a name change -> Tier 5 — Structural Change Event (The compliance program was built for the firm they were, not the firm they just became. A change moment with a hard deadline and no implication of failure.)
6. IF Everything else in the qualified TAM -> Tier 6 — Catch-All: Benchmark (No acute pain to name, so we lead with a number nobody else can publish: what firms at their AUM band actually spend per rep, and how many alerts they review a month.)

Remediation Evidence (post-action)

The strongest signal in the entire market and the most delicate to write. Never quote the fine amount, never name an individual respondent, never characterise conduct — reference the category of finding only. The premise is always 'proving the new program is followed', never 'your old one failed'. This tier is rolling, not a one-time load: it replenishes weekly from enforcement publications.

Filter
Firms appearing in SEC administrative proceedings, FINRA monthly disciplinary summaries or state securities orders in the last 12 months, or carrying a new ADV Item 11 disclosure.
AI strategy
Branch the copy by finding category, not by firm. Advertising findings point at the marketing module; recordkeeping and off-channel findings point at communications; supervision findings point at account surveillance and the testing program. The AI reads the published action, classifies the category, and selects the matching value prop and artifact.

Stack Consolidation (displacement)

Pure displacement. The named tool proves budget exists and the category is understood. The pitch is never 'we are better' — it is 'you are paying four vendors to produce one audit trail'. Highest-leverage moment to send is roughly a quarter before a renewal date.

Filter
Firms where a compliance job post, ADV Part 2A brochure or website positively names an incumbent tool. Absence is never used as a trigger.
AI strategy
Scrape compliance job descriptions and brochure text for named vendors, then name them back in the first line alongside a second tool or consultant from a different source. The AI computes the seat-cost delta at the firm's actual headcount so the money line is specific rather than generic.

Dual-Hat CCO (capacity)

The largest high-quality tier and the most emotionally resonant. These buyers are not shopping — they are surviving. Copy validates the load and offers a number. Firms where ADV Item 1.J(2) shows an outsourced CCO route out of this tier entirely and into the consultant partner motion, because an outsourced CCO firm is a channel, not a target.

Filter
ADV Item 1.J CCO name matched against Schedule A officer titles — CCO also listed as CEO, COO, CIO or Owner. Plus low compliance-headcount ratios and open compliance reqs at sub-250-person firms.
AI strategy
The single most under-used public cross-reference in this market. One join between two parts of the same filing surfaces a person whose compliance title is a legal designation stacked on top of their real job. The AI states the two titles back plainly and offers a load calculator instead of a demo.
Value
Save time

Marketing Rule Exposure (volume)

Volume pain, not risk pain. The signal is generous: testimonials on an RIA site are themselves a post-2021 Marketing Rule artifact requiring specific disclosure treatment. The firms that feel this most are the ones where marketing has stopped asking, because review takes a week.

Filter
Site carries testimonials, third-party ratings, performance figures or award badges, or publishes two or more pieces a month, AND the ADV lists two or more social handles.
AI strategy
Scrape the firm's own live public assets and count them in the first line — testimonial count, award badge, publishing cadence, registered social handles. Link one real asset. The scan offered is generated from their own public pages, so the artifact is genuinely personalized before any conversation happens.

Change Event (new registrant / M&A)

A change moment with a hard deadline attached and no implication of failure — the friendliest tier to write. Newly SEC-registered firms face their first examination cycle under a regime they have never been through; acquirers must merge two sets of supervised persons under one CCO signature.

Filter
Month-over-month ADV deltas: state-to-SEC transitions, branch-count jumps of two or more, name changes and merger filings in the last 12 months.
AI strategy
Two copy branches off one tier. New SEC registrants get the first-exam angle; acquirers and multi-branch expanders get the two-programs-pretending-to-be-one angle. The AI picks the branch from which delta fired, and Allworth Financial is name-dropped specifically on the acquisitive branch.

42% of TAM. The entire tier depends on one asset that does not exist yet — the benchmark itself — which makes it the highest-leverage thing Hadrius can build for this campaign. The ask is to receive the benchmark, not to take a call.

There is no pain to name, so the offer becomes the personalization. Group the TAM into AUM bands from ADV Item 5.F, and offer each firm the cost-per-rep and alert-volume benchmark for its own band. Hadrius can publish this because it runs the systems that produce the numbers; nobody else in the category can.

The rollout

Built once, then fed

Highest-intent tiers first for a fast read, then volume, then the catch-all. After launch the work is loading leads and refreshing the ADV pull — the strategy itself does not get rebuilt.

launch first
01

Phase 0 (weeks -2 to 0) - Infrastructure. Warm sending domains and inboxes, run the first Form ADV bulk pull and BrokerCheck pull, build the field matrix, and load Hadrius's suppression list of current customers and open opportunities.

02

Phase 1 (weeks 1-3) - Proof. Tiers 1 and 2 only, 150-200 contacts a day. Small volume, fast read on whether the filing-based first line lands.

03

Phase 2 (weeks 4-6) - Volume. Add Tier 3 at 400-500 a day. The largest quality tier; expect it to become the workhorse.

04

Phase 3 (weeks 7-9) - Breadth. Add Tiers 4 and 5 at 700-800 a day once the site scrape and change-event deltas are online.

05

Phase 4 (week 10+) - Full TAM. Add Tier 6 at 1,000-1,200 a day. The catch-all absorbs the remaining TAM and the waterfall runs indefinitely on lead top-up.

06

Phase 5 (week 12+) - Side motions. Only once the waterfall is stable: the outsourced-CCO partner channel and the new-CCO-in-seat trigger.

07

Weekly maintenance forever after: append new enforcement actions to Tier 1, append new compliance job posts to Tiers 2 and 3, refresh the ADV pull monthly and re-route any account whose fields changed.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Excellent. Scale this tier hard and widen the trigger definition.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test one copy variant, usually the lead magnet or the notice line.

Negative sentiment above 0.5% Stop. Compliance officers are a small, connected community with active peer forums. Reputation damage here is not recoverable by swapping domains.
