Prepared for Parallel Learning

Your Parallel Learning outbound journey.

One waterfall across every K-12 district in America, routed on public records the districts publish themselves.

Core insight: Special education is the most disclosure-regulated function in American public education. Districts publish what they are buying and from whom (board minutes name the incumbent vendor, the award amount and the term end date), where they are failing (state SPP/APR reports publish LEA-level Indicator 11 - the percentage of initial evaluations completed inside the legal timeline), and who the buyer is (state DOEs publish special education director directories with work emails, because they are public records). The TAM, the routing signal and the verified contact all come from the same free sources. No competitor can buy this advantage, because it lives in the assembly, not the data.

01

The signal

Route on what kind of hole the district is legally obligated to fill - and read it straight out of the public record.

strongest signal
01

It is published by law. Board minutes, procurement portals and state SPP/APR reports are open records - the signal cannot be paywalled, resold or taken away.

02

It carries the contact with it. State DOE special education director directories give a verified name, title, district and work email at zero enrichment cost and near-zero bounce risk.

03

It names the competitor and the clock. Public bid records show which vendor won, for how much, and when the contract expires - so the re-bid window is a date, not a guess.

04

It maps one-to-one onto the product. Indicator 11 is literally the percentage of initial evaluations completed on time. A district under 100% has a state-documented backlog, which is the assessment service described in the state's own words.

Adopted budget PDFs + cooperative purchasing member lists

ESSER-funded position hangover, SPED line items, and which LEAs can purchase through an existing vehicle without running an RFP.

02

The waterfall

One first-match cascade. Every LEA in the country exits with exactly one tier assignment and nothing is left uncovered.

first match wins 
1. IF A live RFP for special education related services is posted, OR an awarded contract expires within the next 180 days -> Tier 1 - Open Procurement (They are already buying this. The only question is from whom, the incumbent is named in the public record, and the decision has a date on it.)
2. IF Indicator 11 is below 100%, OR the LEA determination is anything other than Meets Requirements, OR there is a named state complaint or due process decision -> Tier 2 - Documented Service Gap (The state has published, in its own words, that initial evaluations are running past the legal timeline. That is the assessment product, and the exposure compounds daily.)
3. IF An open req for a school psychologist, SLP or special education teacher has been live 45+ days, or has been reposted -> Tier 3 - Unfillable Role (They have the budget and the posting and still cannot hire. The caseload did not pause - it was absorbed by staff already at capacity.)
4. IF The district is a cooperative purchasing member, OR special education positions were previously ESSER-funded -> Tier 4 - Funding and Purchasing Path (Not a need problem, a procedure problem. They believe adding a provider means running an RFP, so they defer it another year.)
5. IF IDEA child count per special education FTE is above the state median, OR the locale code is rural or town -> Tier 5 - Structural Ratio Gap (The math does not work. There is no local provider pool to hire from at any salary. This is geography, not budget or effort.)
6. IF None of the above -> Catch-all - Role-Based (No individual signal, but the universal one still applies: more students qualifying every year, the same number of people to serve them. The artifact does the work here.)

Indicator 11 below 100%, an LEA determination other than Meets Requirements, or a named state complaint or due process decision.

Pain

Reply density

03 22% TAM

Tier 3: Unfillable Role

Open req for a school psychologist, SLP or special education teacher aged 45+ days - or reposted.

Pain
They have the budget and the posting and still cannot hire. Meanwhile the caseload is being absorbed by people who were already over capacity, and the open line is not saving what it appears to save once make-up services and overtime land.
Offer
Coverage-vs-Hire Cost Model - the true cost of an unfilled req including comp time, contracted make-up services and compensatory exposure, against contracted virtual coverage
Source
Frontline Recruiting, EdJoin, K12JobSpot, SchoolSpring, district HR pages - tracked by posting date and repost
Reply density
Highest raw reply volume. Cleanest signal in the waterfall, and August to September is the window when the pain is loudest.
04 14% TAM

Member of a cooperative purchasing vehicle, or special education positions previously funded by ESSER.

Pain
Not a need problem - a procedure problem. They assume bringing on an outside provider means running a procurement process mid-year, which is not realistic, so the decision gets pushed to next budget cycle every budget cycle.
Offer
Procurement Path Map - the three legal routes to bring Parallel on (sub-threshold direct, co-op vehicle, full RFP) with the effort and timeline for each
Source
Cooperative purchasing member lists (BuyBoard, Sourcewell, OMNIA, AEPA, regional ESCs), adopted budget PDFs, board budget presentations
Reply density
Converts on process relief rather than need. Push it January to April, while next-year budgets are being built.
05 18% TAM

IDEA child count per special education FTE above the state median, or a rural / town locale code.

Pain
The math does not work. There is no local provider pool to hire from at any salary, and the director has usually stopped expecting to solve it by hiring. The fallback - a contractor driving in two days a week - means students see whoever is available.

NCES Common Core of Data, EDFacts IDEA child count, state staffing files
Reply density
Large, stable and non-decaying. The benchmark artifact is the whole pitch - it tells them something about themselves they cannot easily calculate.

The campaigns

Each tier is a named campaign with its own trigger, its own artifact, and its own place on the district calendar.

volume + side quests
Niche Niche

Live Bid Entry

The smallest and highest-converting campaign in the waterfall. The offer is not a meeting - it is the bid-ready response pack, sent whether or not Parallel ends up bidding. Worked as a live weekly queue with human review, never as a static sequence.

Filter
An RFP for special education related services, teletherapy or psychoeducational evaluations is currently posted and open.
AI strategy
Pull the RFP number, title and close date from the district procurement portal, then read the prior-year board packet to see how related services were covered before. Open on the specific bid, not the category.
Value
Win the bid
Niche Niche

Renewal Window

The rubric play is the differentiator. Districts that write an outcome requirement into scoring filter the field faster than any credentials section, and it costs them nothing - which is why it gets adopted, and why it favours the vendor with a 98% number.

Filter
A board-approved contract with a named incumbent vendor expires within 180 days, and no re-bid has posted yet.
AI strategy
Lift the award date, vendor name, service scope and term end date straight from board minutes. Open by counting the days to the re-bid decision. Suggest one outcome requirement for the scoring rubric - IEP goal attainment or evaluation turnaround in days.
Value
Displace the incumbent
Focused Focused

Evaluation Backlog Surge

Positioned as surge capacity, not headcount. The second email introduces compensatory services: once an evaluation runs long enough that services were owed and not delivered, the backlog has to be cleared and paid down at the same time. Run it April to June, into the year-end deadline crunch.

Filter
Indicator 11 below 100% in the most recent state SPP/APR district report, or an LEA determination of Needs Assistance or Needs Intervention.
AI strategy
Pull the district's Indicator 11 percentage alongside peers of the same enrollment band. Lead with the peer context, never the accusation - every district in the pull was under, and for the same structural reason.
Value
Clear the backlog

Hold the Caseload

The framing that lands is that the posting stays up. Parallel holds the caseload while the search continues, so nobody on the existing team carries an extra 40 students. Launched first because it is the largest unblocked tier and August to September is when the pain peaks.

Filter
Open school psychologist, SLP or special education teacher req live 45+ days, with repost history flagged as the strongest sub-signal.
AI strategy
Track posting age and repost cycle. Open on the specific role and the specific number of days - the repost is the proof the search is failing, and it is visible without asking.
Value
Save time

The Vacancy Is Not Free

This is the value-prop rotation inside the Tier 3 sequence rather than a separate send. It exists because the first objection to virtual coverage is always cost, and the honest answer is that the vacancy is already being paid for.

Filter
Same trigger as Hold the Caseload - run as the money-angle variant in the Day 4 rotation.

Reframe the open line from budget underspend to real cost: contracted make-up services, overtime for covering staff, and compensatory obligation.

Buy It Without a Bid

Sells the procurement path before the service, because in this market the process is the blocker. Most districts' first engagement lands under the direct-purchase threshold (commonly $25k to $50k) and never goes to bid at all.

Filter
District appears on a cooperative purchasing member list (BuyBoard, Sourcewell, OMNIA, AEPA, regional ESC).
AI strategy
Name the specific co-op the district belongs to. Most directors do not know related services can be brought on through it without a separate RFP.
Value
Remove the blocker
Focused Focused

The ESSER Hole

A dated, documented, and entirely non-accusatory opener. The district made a funding decision under a deadline; the question is simply what happened to the coverage. Push January to April while next-year budgets are live.

Filter
Special education positions coded to ESSER in a prior adopted budget - either moved to general fund or quietly dropped.
AI strategy
Read the adopted budget PDF and count the ESSER-coded special education lines, then check whether they survived into the current fiscal year.
Value
Restore capacity
Broad Broad

Caseload Arithmetic

The opener explicitly absolves the director: this is not a staffing decision, there are only so many licensed school psychologists within driving distance and most already work somewhere. Continuity - the same provider, same time, every week - beats a contractor driving in twice a week.

Filter
IDEA child count divided by special education staff FTE above the state median, or rural / town locale code.
AI strategy
Compute students-per-specialist for every district in the state from NCES and EDFacts, then hand the district its own position against the state median - a number they cannot easily calculate themselves.
Value
Solve the unsolvable
Broad Broad

The Capacity Report

Softest CTA in the waterfall: the report is free and there is nothing to sit through. This tier sets the reply-rate floor everything else is measured against, and validates the state-directory contact data end to end.

Filter
Every remaining special education director in the target states, segmented by state and enrollment band.
AI strategy
No account-level research. Segment on state and district size only, and let the benchmark report carry the value - including the evaluation-turnaround section directors go to first.
Value
Give before asking
Niche Niche

Co-op and ESC Multiplier (parallel motion)

Deliberately marked as NOT part of the waterfall cascade. One contract can serve 20 to 40 member districts, which makes it the highest-leverage motion in the market - and the reason it is called out here is precisely that it does not fit the cascade and would be lost if it were forced into one.

Filter
BOCES, ESCs and regional special education cooperatives - approximately 1,400 accounts.
AI strategy
Different buyer (co-op executive director), different motion (consortium pricing, member-district onboarding), longer cycle. Human-led, never sequenced.
Value
Multiply the contract

The launch

Launch the tier that is largest, cleanest and unblocked - not the one that is most valuable. Tier 3 in week one.

launch first
01

Weeks 1-3 - Launch Tier 3, Unfillable Role. Largest readable tier, cleanest signal, nothing blocking it, and August to September is the window when unfilled positions hurt most. Fastest path to a reply-rate baseline.

02

Weeks 3-5 - Launch the Catch-all, Role-Based. Establishes the floor everything else is measured against, and validates the state-directory contact data end to end.

03

Weeks 5-8 - Launch Tier 5 (Structural Ratio Gap) and Tier 4 (Funding & Purchasing Path). Both are large, stable, non-decaying lists. Tier 4 goes live properly once the sub-threshold entry package is defined.

04

Weeks 8-12 - Launch Tier 2, Documented Service Gap. Highest value, highest risk. Only after a published evaluation turnaround SLA and a named district case study exist, and only after the copy is reviewed line by line against the no-accusation rule.

05

Rolling from week 6 - Stand up Tier 1, Open Procurement, as a live weekly queue rather than a campaign launch. Small volume, human-reviewed, highest conversion.

06

From week 4 - Begin the co-op / BOCES / ESC multiplier motion alongside the waterfall. Human-led, not sequenced, highest contract value in the market.

07

Ongoing - Refresh Tier 1 and Tier 3 weekly. A filled req or a closed RFP is re-routed, not re-sent. Suppress by district, not by contact. Do not send in the last two weeks of June, in July, or between December 20 and January 3.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacted Perfect. Scale the tier hard.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
1 per 450-500 Borderline. Test one copy variant and hold everything else constant.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate under 1% after 1,500+ sends Failing. Cut and rework the tier.
Opens - any number Ignore entirely. District firewalls pre-fetch links, so opens are meaningless here and tracking pixels on public-records correspondence are a liability. Reply rate is the only metric.
