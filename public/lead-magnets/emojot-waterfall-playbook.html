The signal

The reply button is a public confession, and it is timestamped.

Apollo will sell you a restaurant group's head office address. ZoomInfo will sell you the COO's mobile number. Neither will tell you that the Riyadh branch has a one-star review from 3 March about a refund, still unanswered 168 days later, while the Jeddah branch replies to everything inside a day. That gap is the entire Emojot pitch, stated back to the prospect as a checkable fact about their own business, and it has to be scraped, which is exactly why it works.

strongest signal
01

It is free. Owner responses render publicly on Google Maps with a date attached, and the CFPB and CMS files are open federal data with no key required.

02

It is unbuyable. No data vendor sells owner-response rate across a brand's estate, so no competitor is writing this email.

03

It proves the pain rather than asserting it. The prospect cannot argue with a review at their own location that they have not answered in 168 days.

04

It is self-refreshing. Every new Birdeye deployment adds a Tier 1 account, every quarterly CFPB file republishes Tier 2, and every negative review that ages past thirty days promotes an account from Tier 5 into Tier 3.

01

Google Maps place search and reviews scrape (Apify)

The backbone of the whole build. A brand-name and geo-grid search returns the full location set with count, addresses, rating and review volume; the reviews pass then returns every review with its star, its date, and critically the owner response text and date. That single pull produces the qualification gate, the routing signal for Tier 3 and Tier 5, and the lead magnet, for roughly $0.004 a place and $0.50 per thousand reviews.

02

Homepage and subpage HTML scrape, confirmed against BuiltWith

Tier 1's trigger. Vendor scripts and widget footprints for Birdeye, Podium, Yext, Reputation.com, Medallia, Qualtrics, Delighted, AskNicely, Trustpilot, Feefo, Yotpo, Bazaarvoice, Zendesk, Freshdesk and Intercom, sitting in the prospect's own page source. Free, and second-sourced before we ever name a competitor in copy.

03

CFPB Consumer Complaint Database API

Tier 2 for US financial services. Per-institution complaint counts, product mix, company response and timely-response rate, published by a federal regulator and updated continuously. Open API, no key, no cost. The peer median we compare against is computed from the same file.

04

CMS Care Compare and HCAHPS files

Tier 2 for US healthcare. Patient-experience star ratings by facility, including responsiveness of hospital staff, communication, and willingness to recommend. Free federal data, republished quarterly with the provider's name on it.

05

TripAdvisor and Booking.com property pages

The hospitality equivalent of the Maps signal. Management-response rate and response latency per property, which is often visible where a hotel group's Google presence is fragmented across franchisees.

06

Job boards and LinkedIn Sales Navigator

Tier 4's trigger, refreshed weekly. Open roles for Customer Experience Manager, Guest Relations, Patient Experience, Complaints Officer, Voice of Customer and Service Quality, plus the job-change alerts that feed the new-in-seat side campaign.

07

Store-locator page scrape

The authoritative location count where Maps is ambiguous, and the corporate versus franchise split that decides whether the head office can actually buy for the estate.

08

Apollo and Sales Navigator

The contact layer only. Names, titles and emails, pulled after an account has already qualified and routed. Nothing is enriched before it earns enrichment, which is what keeps the unit economics of a 72,000-account programme intact.

The cascade

One ladder, ordered by how expensive their evidence already is.

Emojot is horizontal across seven industries, which invites the worst possible outbound structure: one campaign per vertical, rebuilt quarterly, none of them compounding. Instead every account in the market is tested against one question. How much public, timestamped, unresolved evidence of experience failure is this organisation carrying, and how much is that evidence already costing them? The industry then decides only two things: which list we pull from, and which case study goes in the email.

first match wins 
1. IF NOT (location_count >= 8 OR total_reviews_estate >= 1000) -> EXCLUDE (Below the gate, Emojot is arguing about price with Birdeye and losing the unified-platform story. The OR clause is deliberate: a 400-bed hospital, a 300-room resort or a university is one location but generates more experience surface area than forty coffee shops, and Emojot's own Radisson and Marino Beach references are single-property.)
2. IF incumbent_tool_detected != none -> TIER 1 - Stack Displacement (Budget is proven and already spent, which makes this the shortest path to a decision. It is also the tier with the strongest arithmetic: per-location review platforms run $299 to $449 per location per month, so a 120-site operator is paying roughly $430,000 a year for the review layer alone.)
3. IF regulator_dataset_hit != none AND regulator_metric_value < regulator_peer_median -> TIER 2 - Published Failure (The failure is not our claim and not their opinion. It is on a federal website with their name on it, and it gets republished next quarter whether or not anything changes in between. Whoever owns that number has a dated problem and usually a budget already attached to it.)
4. IF owner_response_rate_pct < 40 OR aged_unanswered_negatives >= 3 -> TIER 3 - The Open Ledger (Complaints are landing in public and dying there. Every unanswered one-star is a permanent, indexed, dated defect that suppresses local ranking and pre-empts the next booking. Nobody in the business owns it because nobody is watching all the locations at the same time.)
5. IF hiring_experience_role == TRUE OR expansion_signal == TRUE -> TIER 4 - Solving It With Headcount (They have already decided experience is a priority and are buying the most expensive possible version of the answer: a person, with no system underneath them. Reaching them before the hire starts is worth far more than reaching them after.)
6. IF ELSE -> TIER 5 - The Consistency Gap (catch-all) (Multi-site, decent ratings, no public failure, no detected tool. Nothing is on fire, which is exactly the problem: the variance between their best and worst location is invisible internally and obvious externally, and they have no instrument that would flag drift until the rating has already moved.)

The tiers

Five tiers, exclusive routing, and a catch-all that leaves nothing behind.

Tier 1 is already paying a vendor. Tier 2 has a federal regulator publishing its failure rate. Tier 3 has unanswered one-stars sitting in public. Tier 4 is solving the problem by hiring a person and giving them a spreadsheet. Tier 5 has nothing on fire, which is its own problem. Every tier clears the 1,500-contact statistical floor several times over, so each one can be read independently rather than guessed at.

The experiments

Ten campaigns, every one anchored to something already public.

No campaign here needs a data purchase or a discovery call to run. Each one is built from a review corpus, a federal file, a job posting, or a vendor script sitting on the prospect's own website. The most expensive input in the entire build is a few dollars of scraping credit.

The Per-Location Tax (economics-led)

The single sharpest argument available in the whole build, made entirely in the prospect's own numbers. A 120-site operator is paying roughly $430,000 a year for reviews alone, and the bill grows every time they open a location. Emojot is not priced that way and covers four more modules besides.

Filter
Birdeye, Podium, Yext or Reputation.com detected on site, and location_count above 40.
AI strategy
Count the estate from the Maps scrape, then price their current review layer against published per-location rates of $299 to $449 per location per month. Put the annual figure in the second email rather than the first, so the opener earns the read and the arithmetic closes it.

The Gap Between Systems (coverage-led)

Displacement without attacking the incumbent. The pitch is not that their tool is bad, it is that their tool was never designed to catch the complaint that arrives by WhatsApp at branch 44. Barista runs 61 cafes with 11,000+ reviews and 450+ complaints on one platform instead of four.

Any incumbent tool detected, with the tool class recorded as review, survey, enterprise_xm or helpdesk.

Tier 1 and Tier 3 together, then build outward.

Tier 1 is the highest-intent, fastest-proof tier and needs only the vendor footprint scrape. Tier 3 is the largest instrumented tier and proves the Maps pipeline and the magnet at volume. Running them together in phase one validates both halves of the thesis before a dollar goes into the regulatory ingest.

02

Weeks 5 to 8, phase two: add Tier 2 once the CFPB and CMS ingests are built and the peer medians are computed from the same files. US only at first, because that is the only market where the regulator publishes the signal.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Perfect. Scale that tier immediately and add domains behind it.
1 per 300 Good. Let it run untouched.
