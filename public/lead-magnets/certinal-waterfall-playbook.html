02 Routing

First match wins, top to bottom

Each account is tested against the rules in order and lands in the first tier it matches. No account sits in two tiers, and the final tier catches everything the others did not.

first match wins 
1. IF An incumbent eSignature vendor is detected AND headcount is 1,000 or more -> Tier 1, Enterprise Displacement (At this scale they sit on a multi-year seat contract, and the renewal uplift is a procurement problem with a named owner.)
2. IF An incumbent eSignature vendor is detected AND headcount is 100 to 999 -> Tier 2, Mid-Market Displacement (Same signal, smaller wallet, sharper pain. Seat economics and envelope expiry hurt proportionally more at this size.)
3. IF The account is a healthcare provider AND an EHR is detected OR a downloadable patient forms page exists -> Tier 3, Healthcare e-Consent (Publishing consent PDFs for download is direct evidence the signing loop is still paper, and this tier carries the strongest hard proof Certinal owns.)
4. IF A regulatory framework is published on a trust page OR a compliance, privacy or data protection officer is in post -> Tier 4, Cross-Border Compliance (They have publicly committed to a posture, which makes signature defensibility rather than price the live exposure.)
5. IF Headcount is 250 or more AND Legal, Procurement, HR or Contract Ops is present -> Tier 5, Catch-All and Agreement Volume (High agreement volume with no visible vendor commitment usually means signing is fragmented across departments and geographies, not absent.)

03 Tiers

Five tiers across the whole market

Tiers 1 and 2 split one signal by wallet size. Tiers 3 and 4 capture regulated workflows where the signature itself, not its price, is the exposure. Tier 5 takes the rest.

06 Launch

Tier 1 first, catch-all last

Prove the displacement thesis on the smallest, hottest list before committing volume spend. Then phase the rest in over twelve weeks.

launch first
01

Weeks 1 to 3, Tier 1 only. Smallest and hottest list, giving the fastest read on whether the displacement message lands before any volume spend is committed.

02

Weeks 3 to 6, add Tiers 2 and 3. Tier 2 tests whether the displacement message survives a smaller wallet. Tier 3 runs in parallel on different proof and different sending domains, so it reads as a clean second signal rather than a dilution.

03

Weeks 6 to 9, add Tier 4. Layer in the compliance angle once we know which proof points are actually pulling replies.

04

Weeks 9 to 12, add Tier 5. The volume tier goes last and borrows whichever value proposition tested strongest in Tiers 1 and 2.

05

Week 12 onward, add the two side campaigns. Review-site reviewer intercept on anyone who reviewed DocuSign or Adobe Sign in the last 90 days, and a renewal-window trigger on newly appointed Procurement, Legal Ops and Contract Ops leaders.

06

Before the first send: close the three open gaps, size sending infrastructure for the 267,000-contact catch-all, build all five magnets, and suppress existing Zycus customers and open opportunities against the full list.

07

Run the healthcare tier on separate sending domains from the displacement tiers, so a deliverability problem in one cannot contaminate the other.

Scale / cut rules

1 positive reply per 150 contacts Excellent. Scale the tier hard, raise daily volume and expand geography.
1 per 300 Good. Leave it alone and let it run untouched.
1 per 450 to 500 Borderline. Test one copy variant, changing the magnet or the first-line research angle only.
Worse than 1 per 500, or reply rate below 1 percent after 1,500 sends Failing. Cut the tier and rework it. Two variants per tier is the ceiling, so results stay readable.
